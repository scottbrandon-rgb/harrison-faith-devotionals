## Devotional

In Egypt, water came out of a ditch. You cut the channel, you worked the pump with your feet, you moved the river to the row you wanted, and the field got watered when you told it to. But water in Canaan fell out of the sky.

God said as they crossed the Jordan, the land you are going to take "is not like the land of Egypt, from which you have come, where you sowed your seed and irrigated it, like a garden of vegetables," because Canaan is "a land of hills and valleys, which drinks water by the rain from heaven" (Deuteronomy 11:10, 11). He took a people out of a land they could schedule and put them in a land they had to trust, on purpose. Everything they ate for the rest of their lives depended on whether they believed He would send rain.

So when God calls Himself "the fountain of living waters" (Jeremiah 2:13), He is talking about moving water. Living water runs. It comes to you on its own, it arrives on His timing, and you cannot store it or ration it or decide when it shows up. A cistern is the other arrangement entirely. You cut it out of the limestone in your own yard, you seal the walls, you catch what falls, and from that day forward you draw when you feel like drawing.

That is the difference between a Savior and a solution. A Savior is someone you look to. A solution is something you craft. You can schedule a cistern, and you cannot schedule rain, so when God does not answer the way you wanted or on the calendar you wanted, the chisel starts to look reasonable. It looks like initiative. It looks like being an adult about your problems. Every cistern in Israel started as a good idea.

Watch how the story finishes. Years later, when the solutions failed and Israel finally cried out in their misery, God answered them: "Go and cry out to the gods whom you have chosen; let them save you in the time of your distress" (Judges 10:14). He sends them back to the thing they trusted and lets the cistern prove itself. Jeremiah records the same verdict, that the gods they made should "arise, if they can save you, in your time of trouble" (Jeremiah 2:28).

That is the terrifying part of this whole series. If you are hungry, if you are sick, if you are broke, if the relationship is falling apart, God has every right to send you to whatever you have been drawing from. Nobody wakes up and decides to forsake God. We just keep finding things that work without Him.

**Ask yourself today whether you're looking for a Savior or shopping for a solution.**

## Application

1. Where in your life have you stopped asking God for something because you already worked out how to get it yourself?
2. What is one thing you could hand back to God this week that you have been handling on your own?

## Challenge

Take the situation you have already solved without God and give it back to Him today, out loud, before you take one more step on it. Tell Him what you built, tell Him why you built it, and ask Him what He wants to do with it instead. A cistern only holds what you put in it, so stop drawing from yours long enough to find out whether the rain still falls.

## Prayer

Father, You are the fountain of living waters, and I have spent years cutting out places to hold my own. I did it because Your timing scared me and my own hands felt safer. Show me the solutions I built while I was still calling You my Savior. Teach me to live in a land that drinks from heaven, and give me the patience to wait on rain I cannot schedule.
