## Devotional

Iron chariots are a weapon in the valley. They need flat ground and open road, and in broken hill country they are close to useless. So when Judah "took possession of the hill country, but he could not drive out the inhabitants of the plain because they had chariots of iron" (Judges 1:19), what actually happened is that Judah fought where the enemy was weakest and stopped when the ground leveled out.

God had already used the word land and meant all of it. "Judah shall go up; behold, I have given the land into his hand" (Judges 1:2). Joshua had already settled the chariot question too. When the sons of Joseph came to him with this exact complaint, he granted every bit of it and told them to go anyway: "you shall drive out the Canaanites, though they have chariots of iron, and though they are strong" (Joshua 17:18). The chariots were real, but the chariots were also beside the point.

Read the rest of Judges 1 and watch the pattern. Ephraim did not drive them out. Zebulun did not. Asher did not. Naphtali did not. Dan could not even hold the ground he was handed. The subject of every one of those sentences is Israel, not the weakness of God.

The hill country was comfortable, and comfort compromises conviction. Comfort told them they were blessed, because look at all the land they took. Comfort told them they were obedient, because look at all the enemies they drove out. Comfort told them the plains could wait, because life is good up here where nothing has wheels.

Then verse 28 arrives and takes the excuse away. "When Israel grew strong, they put the Canaanites to forced labor, but did not drive them out completely" (Judges 1:28). The strength came but the obedience didn't. God had told them exactly what leaving people in the land would cost them, that those they let remain would be "barbs in your eyes and thorns in your sides" (Numbers 33:55), and they stayed up in the hills anyway. Oh how we love our comfortable ways of living.

So what is the plain in your life? Somewhere in you there is a sin you quit fighting, and you quit so quietly that you could not tell me the year it happened. You could not drive out the anger, so you learned to live with it. You could not drive out the lust, so you learned to live with it. You could not drive out what you feel toward the person who hurt you, so you learned to live with it. Now you have a word for it that is softer than sin. You call it your personality, or your struggle, or the way you have always been. Here's what I know and I hope it stirs you like it has me;

Familiarity is the anesthetic of obedience. 

**Go back to the last thing that stopped bothering you and start fighting it again today.**

## Application

1. Where in your life has something stopped bothering you that used to keep you awake at night?
2. What is one step you could take this week against something you quietly agreed to live with?

## Challenge

Pick the one thing you made peace with and say it out loud to God using the real word for it instead of the softer word you have been using. Then tell one person you trust that you are done living beside it. Familiarity survives on privacy, so the first blow you land is refusing to keep it quiet.

## Prayer

Father, I made peace with things You told me to drive out, and I did it so slowly that I never noticed the day I stopped. Show me the plain I have been avoiding while I congratulate myself over the hill country. I am not asking for an easy fight, because You never promised Judah one either. Give me the faith to go down into the ground I have been avoiding, and wake up the part of me that used to care.
