## Devotional

One generation! That is the whole gap. "There arose another generation after them who did not know the LORD or the work that he had done for Israel" (Judges 2:10). Their parents watched the Jordan stand still and the walls come down at Jericho. The children grew up looking at altars in the yard. The parents were not bowing at those altars, which is what makes this so unnerving. 

They kept the LORD. They just kept everything else too. 

Then comes verse 12, that "they abandoned the LORD, the God of their fathers, who had brought them out of the land of Egypt. They went after other gods, from among the gods of the peoples who were around them." Israel never took God off the list. They kept adding to it. Addition is why nobody repents, because removing God from your house would wreck you, and setting something up beside Him costs you nothing that you can tell.

While I was studying this, I kept having people's names come to mind, people I was certain the Lord wanted to speak to. He scrubbed every one of them off my heart and told me this sermon was aimed at me. That is the exact trouble in Jeremiah 2. God brings the indictment and Israel cannot tell He is talking about them. They answer Him with, "I am innocent; surely his anger has turned from me" (Jeremiah 2:35), and they are not being clever. They actually believe it.

So I asked what altars were still standing in my own house, and the answer was lies. I used lies to explain why my life was hard. I used lies to manufacture worth I did not feel. I used lies to move people into accepting me. Every one of those lies worked, and that is the whole problem, because a lie that works is a cistern. It holds just enough water to keep you from ever going to the fountain.

What settled it was my girls. Whatever I leave standing, I don't even have to teach them. They will receive it as normal the way Israel's children received altars they never had to build, and I do not want my children wrestling the same lies I have wrestled. 

What we fail to tear down, our children will worship. In fact I would go so far as saying, "What we become familiar with, is what our children become forced labors to."

Now...notice where the angel of the LORD walks in this chapter. He goes "up from Gilgal to Bochim" (Judges 2:1). Gilgal is where the reproach of Egypt was rolled away (Joshua 5:9), where covenant was cut into a whole generation, where God marked a people as His own. Bochim means weepers. They lifted up their voices, they wept, they even offered sacrifices there (Judges 2:4, 5), and every altar in the land was still standing when they went home.

Your house lives at one of those two addresses. Gilgal is where things get cut off. Bochim is where things get cried over. God said it plainly through Joel, "rend your hearts and not your garments" (Joel 2:13), because a family can grieve the consequences of sin for years and never once grieve the sin.

**Tear the altar down, don't just weep over it's existence.**

## Application

1. What is standing in your house right now that you have apologized for but never actually removed?
2. What is one thing you could end, delete, cancel, or confess this week so that your children never inherit it?

## Challenge

Pick one altar and take it all the way down today in a way that costs you something. Delete the account, make the phone call, tell your spouse the truth, close the door you have been leaving cracked. Weeping over it is Bochim, and Bochim never changed a thing for anybody who stood there.

## Prayer

Father, I kept You and I kept other things beside You, and not once did it feel like leaving. Show me the altars in my house that I have learned to walk past without seeing. Give me the honesty to tear them down instead of crying about them, and let my children inherit a house with one altar in it. Roll away my reproach the way You rolled away Israel's at Gilgal, and let this be the place where covenant gets cut again.
