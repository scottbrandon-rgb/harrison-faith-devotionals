## Devotional

In the ancient world, forced labor was ordinary business. A conquering people would leave a subject population right where it was and conscript it for the heavy work, the quarrying and the hauling and the building, and everyone understood the arrangement. The subject people kept their lives. The conquerors kept their backs.

So when "Israel grew strong, they put the Canaanites to forced labor, but did not drive them out completely" (Judges 1:28), Israel was doing what every nation in that region did. The moment they finally had the power to obey, they used it to profit. Four centuries later Solomon was still running the same system on the same leftover peoples, "these Solomon drafted to be slaves, and so they are to this day" (1 Kings 9:21). It's been said, "What one generation keeps for convenience, a later generation inherits as policy."

How did Israel even know the Canaanite was worth keeping? Familiarity taught them. You cannot live beside something for twenty years without learning what it is good for. The longer it stays, the more useful it looks, until the thing God told you to destroy is the thing holding up your economy.

Do not pretend sin has nothing to offer you. James says "each person is tempted when he is lured and enticed by his own desire," and "desire when it has conceived gives birth to sin, and sin when it is fully grown brings forth death" (James 1:14, 15). Desire is what assigns worth. Nothing in your life ever gets used until something in you first decides it is worth using, and that decision is the whole ballgame.

Here is where the exchange happens. The more you use it, the more you get out of it. The more you get out of it, the more it is worth to you. The more it is worth to you, the more of your life it gets to run. Adam walked that same road in a garden. He valued something more than he valued the word of God, and the dominion he handed over is the dominion he came under (Genesis 3:6, 17). A good gift will do this to you just as fast as a sin will, the moment you depend on it more than you depend on the Giver.

The value we assign is the authority we surrender. 

Israel never signed a treaty handing the land back. They just found the Canaanite useful, and useful turned into necessary, and necessary is a kind of ownership.

So ask the honest question about the gods of this culture. 
Have they become acceptable inside your house because you could not drive them out in public? 
How much are you letting benefit you privately because you went numb to it publicly?

**Before you use it again, ask what it is charging you.**

## Application

1. Where in your life have you started getting something useful out of a thing God told you to get rid of?
2. What benefit could you give up this week that you only have because you kept something you should have removed?

## Challenge

Write down what your compromise actually pays you. The comfort, the income, the escape, the attention, the way it keeps a hard conversation from happening, put it in plain words on paper. You will not tear anything down until you admit what it has been doing for you.

## Prayer

Father, I took things You told me to destroy and I put them to work for me. I called it wisdom, and it was really a trade. Show me what I have been getting out of it, and show me what it has been getting out of me. I would rather be poorer and free than useful to something that owns me.
