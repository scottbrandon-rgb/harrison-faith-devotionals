## Devotional

A cistern, is not a hole in the ground but a chamber cut down into bedrock, wide at the bottom and narrow at the mouth so less water evaporates off the top and less filth falls in. Limestone is porous, so the entire inside has to be coated in lime plaster or the water walks out through the walls. Then the ground shifts, the plaster dries and splits, and what you saved bleeds away into rock.

That is weeks of work, swinging a chisel down in a pit you cut yourself, for water you still have to ration. People do not do that unless the alternative feels worse, and Israel decided waiting on God felt worse.

Watch the moment God says so. The angel of the LORD reminds them of the covenant, that "you shall make no covenant with the inhabitants of this land; you shall break down their altars," and then delivers the charge, "But you have not obeyed my voice. What is this you have done?" And continues in verse 3. "So now I say, I will not drive them out before you" (Judges 2:2, 3).

I will not drive them out before you. By this point Israel had no real use for that anyway. Driving the Canaanite out was God's work, and Israel had already found a better return on keeping them employed. They traded the need for God to remove an enemy for the need to keep that enemy productive. The need to deal with the enemy did not change, but the dependency did.

That is what makes this so quiet. You will not ask God for something you have already arranged. The petition dries up first, then the dependence, then the relationship, and none of it ever feels like walking away from Him. It feels like finally getting your life together. Nobody announces a departure. They just stop needing Him for the one thing He promised to do. 

For a need that you do not see, results in a God that you do not seek.

Israel is exactly the person God describes in Jeremiah, the one who says "I am innocent; surely his anger has turned from me" (Jeremiah 2:35). They meant it. They could not hear the indictment because nothing in their life felt like rebellion. It felt like management.

Every cistern in Judah started as an answer to a real problem, and so did yours. The chisel work felt responsible. The plaster felt like stewardship. Then it cracked, the way they all crack, because water was never meant to be hoarded by people who live under rain.

**Find the need you stopped praying about and start seeking God in prayer today.**

## Application

1. When did you last ask God for something you have since figured out how to get without Him?
2. What is one prayer you could start praying again this week, even though you already have the situation handled?

## Challenge

Go back to your prayers from a year ago, whether they are written in a journal or just sitting in your memory, and find the one that quietly disappeared. Pray it today word for word the way you prayed it then. Then ask God to show you what you built in the meantime that made you stop.

## Prayer

Father, I stopped asking You for things because I found ways to get them. I called it maturity, and it was really independence. Crack the plaster on the cisterns I have been drinking from and let me feel the thirst I have been managing so well. I want to need You for the things You promised to do, and I want to want them from Your hand.
