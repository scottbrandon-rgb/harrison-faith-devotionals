## Devotional

The whole book turns on one small word, and you'd skip right over it. "Though the fig tree should not blossom" (Habakkuk 3:17). Though, which is the old way of saying suppose, or even if.

Most of us only use that word at 2 in the morning. Suppose the biopsy comes back the way I'm afraid it will. Suppose he doesn't come home this time. Suppose the job goes. Suppose we bury another one. We use suppose to rehearse dread in the dark and then lie there staring at the ceiling.

Habakkuk does something braver than that. He invites suppose into the house, sits it down at his own table, and builds the worst thing he can think of on purpose.

And his list isn't random. Fig, vine, olive, grain, flock, herd. That is the entire economy of Judah, every food source and every income source in one breath. It gets worse when you know that the fig tree and the vine were the standing picture of a person safe and blessed in the land, every man sitting "under his vine and under his fig tree" (1 Kings 4:25). Worse still when you remember Deuteronomy 28, where God promised those exact things to a people who obeyed Him.

So read what he's really saying. Suppose every visible sign that You are pleased with me is gone. Suppose the blessings You promised are stripped off the land and there's nothing left in my hands to prove You're for me.

Then comes the hinge. "Yet I will rejoice in the LORD; I will take joy in the God of my salvation" (3:18).

That's the same posture 3 Hebrew men took in front of a furnace. Our God is able to deliver us, "but if not, be it known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods" (Daniel 3:18). But if not, same word and the same spine. They settled it before they knew the outcome, which is the only time settling it counts for anything.

Here's what I want you to see. Habakkuk finished the sentence. Most of us start with suppose and stop, and we leave ourselves lying there in the worst case with no God at the end of it. He walked all the way through the worst thing and found God still standing on the other side of it, and that is the difference between rehearsing dread and actually trusting Somebody.

You've got a suppose. You've had it for weeks. Take it all the way to the end of the sentence and put God there.

**Finish the sentence that starts with suppose, and put God at the end of it.**

## Application

1. What is the suppose you've been rehearsing at 2 in the morning without ever finishing it?
2. What would it take for you to say that whole sentence out loud today, ending with "yet I will rejoice in the LORD"?

## Challenge

Write out your worst case in one sentence, the real one, the one you don't say to people. Then write "yet I will rejoice in the LORD" underneath it in your own handwriting and read the whole thing out loud. Do it before the situation resolves, because after it resolves it doesn't cost you anything.

## Prayer

God, I've been supposing for weeks and I never once let You into the sentence. I've built the worst thing a hundred times in my head at night and left You out of every version of it. So here it is, all the way to the end, and You're still there when I get to the bottom of it. Even if none of it turns, I will rejoice in You. You're the God of my salvation and that part isn't up for a vote.
