## Devotional

Three verses ago this man's legs were shaking underneath him. Now read where he ends up. "GOD, the Lord, is my strength; he makes my feet like the deer's; he makes me tread on my high places" (Habakkuk 3:19).

Two things come out of the choice he made, and neither one of them is a solved problem.

The first is joy. Happiness runs on circumstances, so when your stuff is up you're up and when your stuff goes south you go south with it, which is why happiness has always felt like a roller coaster you didn't ask to ride. Joy is inner stability that holds steady regardless of what's happening outside of you. Happiness comes from the things that just failed you. Joy comes from the God who cannot fail, and He hasn't changed since the last time you checked. That's why Paul could write "Rejoice in the Lord always" from a jail cell and mean it (Philippians 4:4).

The second is strength, and Habakkuk gives you a picture for it. Feet like the deer's. He's watching a mountain deer work a slope that would stop anything else alive, hooves that spread and grip, jumping rock to rock, cutting sideways around what it can't go through, climbing terrain that has no business being climbed. Then, "he makes me tread on my high places."

Look at that picture for a second. The mountain is still there. Nothing about the mountain moved. What changed is that the thing that was walking all over him is now underneath his feet, and he's on top of it looking down.

David wrote the same line generations earlier, "He made my feet like the feet of a deer and set me secure on the heights" (Psalm 18:33). Habakkuk went and got a song out of the songbook and used it as the last word of the darkest book he ever wrote.

Which brings us to the one line everybody reads past. "To the choirmaster: with stringed instruments." He took it to the worship team. The tired, confused, angry, frightened prophet took the record of his worst season, set it to music, and handed it to the congregation to sing.

He never got his questions answered. Read it again if you don't believe me, because God never explains Himself anywhere in this book. What Habakkuk got was God, and it turned out God was the answer he'd been after the whole time.

That's where the empty ends, with a man on a mountain he couldn't climb 3 verses ago, singing. The fig tree never did bloom.

**The mountain stays where it is, and God changes your feet.**

## Application

1. Where have you been waiting on the mountain to move when God has been offering to change your feet?
2. Who is one person you could tell this week about something God carried you through this year?

## Challenge

Pick the one thing that's still unresolved, the thing you'd trade anything to have settled, and worship God over it today before anything about it changes. Sing something, out loud, in the car if that's the only place you're alone. Do it while it's still dark and it'll mean something.

## Prayer

Lord, You are my strength, and I've spent this season asking You to move a mountain instead of asking You to change my feet. You never explained Yourself to Habakkuk and he ended up singing anyway. I don't have my answers either, and I have You, and I'm learning that's the better end of the deal. Put me on the high places, and let me get there climbing.
