## Devotional

A cistern was a hole cut into rock to catch and store water through the dry season. In that part of the world you had to have one. The problem was the rock. Most of it was porous limestone, so the water they worked all winter to collect just seeped out through the walls of the thing that was supposed to hold it.

Around the thirteenth century B.C. they figured out plaster. Line the cistern, seal the walls, stop the seepage. It was a real solution and it worked, and I want you to notice what it never solved. Plaster cracks. The ground shifts, the summer heat expands the rock, a hairline opens somewhere down in the dark, and by August the cistern you spent months on is dry. You do not find out on the day you dig. You find out on the day you're thirsty.

Now hear the foolishness of it. Walking away from a spring that flows year round in order to go dig a cracked hole in the desert. Digging a cracked cistern is building something guaranteed to fail before you ever pick up the shovel, and that is what Israel did, and if we're honest it is what we keep doing. We leave the sustainable, life giving presence of God to go build substitutes with our own hands.

We say work smarter, not harder. Spiritually a lot of us have done the reverse. We are working ourselves into the ground trying to achieve on our own strength what only He can supply, and underneath most of that effort is guilt. Guilt has one sentence and it repeats it all day: I am not enough. Not leading well enough. Not parenting well enough. Not praying well enough. Not keeping the house clean enough. And God's answer to that sentence is not a pep talk about your potential. His answer is, I am your source. You were never supposed to be enough, because you were never the source.

I said this Sunday and I meant it for the leaders reading this. Some of you are volunteers, some of you carry a title, and you are running on fumes and hiding it well. He is not looking to beat you over the head. He said, "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28). If you will not admit you're empty, there is no honest way to get full.

**Stop plastering the cistern and walk back to the spring.**

## Application

1. Where has guilt been doing the driving in your life, telling you that you are not enough?
2. What is one thing you could hand back to God this week that you have been carrying on your own strength?

## Challenge

Say it plainly to God today: I am on empty. Then tell one trustworthy person the same thing, out loud, before the week is out. A leader who cannot admit he is dry will keep pouring until there's nothing left of him.

## Prayer

God, I have been working harder instead of drawing deeper. I have plastered over the cracks and hoped nobody noticed the cistern was empty, and I am tired in a way sleep doesn't touch. Deal with the guilt that has been driving me, because it has never once made me full. You are my source, and today I am asking You to be the one who carries what I have been carrying alone. Amen.
