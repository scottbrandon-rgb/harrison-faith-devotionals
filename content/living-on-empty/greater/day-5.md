## Devotional

Watch the reversal. In verse 16 their eyes were kept. In verse 31 their eyes were opened. Same divine passive, opposite direction, which means the God who veiled them is the God who unveiled them and He picked the moment Himself. And look at the moment He picked. Not during two hours of the finest preaching that road will ever hear. At the supper, when a guest in their house took the bread out of the host's hands, blessed it, broke it, and gave it to them, the same four motions He made in front of five thousand people and in an upper room three nights before. Maybe when His hands came up to break that loaf they finally saw what was in them. Luke does not tell us. Luke tells us the recognition came at the table.

Then verse 31 does something that catches me every time I read it. "And he vanished from their sight." The moment they could finally see Him, He was gone. That vanishing is a promotion. He removed the visible so they could learn to walk with the invisible, because in forty days He ascends, and in fifty days the Spirit falls, and these two men are going to know a Christ who is not seven miles down the road but living inside them. The incarnation had to lead to the resurrection. The resurrection had to lead to the ascension. The ascension had to lead to Pentecost. At every one of those steps the way they had known Him before had to end so the greater way could begin.

Which brings me to the thing I most want you to hear this week. Some of you have been grieving a season and calling it grieving God. You miss youth camp. You miss the first year after you got saved when every worship song wrecked you. You miss when prayer felt like a thunderstorm instead of a discipline, and you have concluded from all of it that He left. He did not leave. He got bigger, and the container you were carrying Him in will not hold Him anymore.

Now read what they said to each other after He vanished. "Did not our hearts burn within us while he talked to us on the road?" Catch when that burning happened. Back on the road, before they recognized Him, while their eyes were still closed. The evidence was inside them for two hours and they could not identify it until afterward. Some of you have already had the burn, in the car, in a hospital hallway, during a song you were not even paying attention to, and you never named it because you were still watching for Him to show up the old way. And a fire that gets kindled is a fire that had gone out. That is the whole story of these two men. The fire was out, the heart was slow, the hope was past tense, and a stranger opened a book and the coals caught again.

Then look at what a kindled heart does. "And they rose that same hour and returned to Jerusalem" (Luke 24:33). That same hour. It is dark now, and they had just told Him the day was far spent, which is the very reason they begged Him to stay. Seven miles back the way they came, at night, on a road with no lights, because revelation is measured in relocation. When your eyes get opened you do not wait for morning. He came down so we could come up. He met us where we were so we could come to where He is.

**He is not gone. He is greater.**

## Application

1. What season have you been grieving and calling it grieving God, and what did the old way of knowing Him give you that you have not let Him replace?
2. Who do you know on the Emmaus road right now, and what would it take for you to go walk seven miles with them this week?

## Challenge

Go back through the last few months and find the burn you never named. The car, the hallway, the song, the verse that landed harder than it should have. Name it out loud as Him. Then get up that same hour and go tell somebody who stopped coming, because you have the two things they need, the Scriptures and a table.

## Prayer

Father, I have missed the old way of knowing You so much that I mistook it for missing You. Thank You that You vanished from their sight only because something greater was coming, and that You have never once taken something away without opening something larger. Kindle what has gone out in me. Let me recognize You at the table, name the burn I have already felt, and get up that same hour to go find somebody walking away. You are not gone. You are greater. In Jesus' name, amen.
