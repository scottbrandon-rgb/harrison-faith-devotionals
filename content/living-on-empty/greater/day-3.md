## Devotional

Jesus gives them the diagnosis in verse 25, and notice where He puts it. "O foolish ones, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken." Not slow of mind. Slow of heart. These men were not stupid and they were not short on information, because they had just recited the facts to Him accurately. Somewhere between Friday afternoon and Sunday morning their believing slowed down, and when the believing slowed down, the seeing shut off.

A slow heart never announces itself. Nobody wakes up and decides to quit believing. It goes the way a fire goes out, one log at a time, and you do not notice until you reach for the warmth and it is not there anymore. That is why these two are seven miles down the wrong road. The geography was only the symptom. The distance that mattered was the one between them and believing.

Then Jesus asks the question that reframes the entire weekend. "Was it not necessary that the Christ should suffer these things and enter into his glory?" (Luke 24:26). That word necessary is carrying enormous weight in Luke's Gospel. It is the word for divine obligation, the thing that must happen because God has determined it, and it is the same word Jesus used at twelve years old when He said He must be in His Father's house, and again in Luke 9 when He said the Son of Man must suffer many things. The cross was never the interruption of the plan. The very thing they had filed away as evidence it failed is the mechanism by which it succeeded.

So let me say this carefully, because I know who is reading. They were sad about the right event and wrong about what it meant. Their reporting was accurate and their conclusion was upside down. The diagnosis is real. The empty chair at the table is real. The prodigal is really gone. I am not asking anyone to pretend otherwise, and I would not insult you by trying. I am asking you to consider that you may be accurately reporting your circumstances and still drawing an upside down conclusion from them, because He suffered first and then entered His glory, and the road through was the only road in.

Now look at how much Bible Jesus preached on that road. Moses, all the Prophets, all the Scriptures, which is the entire Old Testament from Genesis forward, and He is telling two men that every page of it has been about Him the whole time. The Seed of the woman. The Passover Lamb. The Prophet greater than Moses. The Kinsman-Redeemer of Ruth. The suffering Servant of Isaiah 53. Every one of those was written down before Friday, and these men had heard it read in the synagogue their entire lives. Here is what they had done, and it is exactly what we do. They took the last seventy two hours of their experience and used it to interpret the whole Bible. Jesus took the whole Bible and used it to interpret the last seventy two hours.

**Experience will tell you what God has failed to do. Scripture will tell you what God has already done.**

## Application

1. When did your believing start to slow, and what have you been reaching for lately expecting warmth that is not there anymore?
2. What are you interpreting through the last three days of your experience that you need to start interpreting through the whole record of Scripture instead?

## Challenge

Go wider in the book today, not louder. Read past the verses that comfort you and get into the long record, Moses and the prophets and the four thousand year account of a God who has never broken His word one time. You need somewhere to stand that is older than your circumstance, and the coffee mug verses will not hold weight when Friday comes.

## Prayer

Lord, my heart went slow and I never noticed the fire going out. Forgive me for taking the last seventy two hours of my life and using them to interpret Your whole Word, when You gave me Your whole Word to interpret my seventy two hours. Show me where my reporting has been accurate and my conclusion upside down. And where the road through is the only road in, give me the faith to keep walking it. In Jesus' name, amen.
