## Devotional

Three words in verse 21 tell you everything about where these two men are. "We had hoped." That is hope with a funeral already held for it, hope moved into the past tense and filed away with the other things that did not work out. If you have ever caught yourself saying you used to believe God for something, you have spoken their sentence.

Here is what wrecks me about it. He did redeem Israel. He redeemed Israel that weekend more completely, more permanently, and more gloriously than anything those two men had drawn up on their own. Their hope was answered while they were burying it. They wanted Rome pushed out of Jerusalem, and He pushed death out of the grave. They wanted a throne in the city, and He took a throne over the cosmos. They wanted one nation restored, and He opened a kingdom to every nation on the earth. They got more than they asked for, and standing on that road it looked to them like less, because greater always shows up in a form the old expectation cannot hold.

Now look at what Jesus does with two men in that condition. He asks them, "What things?" (Luke 24:19). Sit with how strange and how tender that is. He knows. He was there. It is His own crucifixion they are about to summarize for Him, and He watched every hour of it from the inside. He stands on that road and says, tell Me about it.

Why make them say it out loud when He already knows? Because something happens in you when you put your disappointment into words in the presence of God that never happens while you carry it silently. David worked this way through the Psalms over and over. He turns toward God, he complains, he asks, and then he trusts. The complaint was never the enemy of the trust.

And what comes out of them in the next few verses will humble you. They tell Him about the prophet mighty in deed and word. They tell Him it is now the third day. They even tell Him the women found the tomb empty and the angels said He was alive. They had the resurrection report in their own mouths and they were still walking the wrong direction. Then comes the saddest line on that road, "but him they did not see" (Luke 24:24). Sir, He is standing right there. He is answering your question. You are looking at Him. How many times have I explained my situation to the One who authored it, and prayed a prayer that was really just a briefing?

**Your hope may have already been answered on a scale your old hope cannot recognize.**

## Application

1. Where have you moved a hope into the past tense, and what did you decide about God on the day you buried it?
2. What is the disappointment you have been carrying silently, and who could you say it out loud to this week, starting with God Himself?

## Challenge

Take the one thing you have quietly stopped believing God for and put it into words in His presence today. Not a summary and not a briefing, the actual complaint the way David prayed it. Then ask Him to show you what He has already done in that place that you have not been able to see.

## Prayer

Father, I have prayed like a man giving You a report on a situation You already authored. Forgive me for the hopes I buried while You were answering them, and for deciding You had failed because the answer did not arrive in the shape I drew up. Teach me to bring the complaint to You instead of carrying it alone. And where You have already answered on a scale I cannot see, give me eyes for it. In Jesus' name, amen.
