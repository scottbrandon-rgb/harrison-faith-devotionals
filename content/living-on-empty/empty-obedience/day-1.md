## Devotional

For 3 weeks I have been telling you "the empty was not your fault."
First week, A hidden face is not an absent God. 
Second week, A poured-out life is never a wasted one. 
Last week, Two men walked all the way to Emmaus with Jesus beside them and never knew it. 

All of that is true, but there is a second question: Why am I empty, and what part of this do "I" own?

When we read Jonah, we see he got a second chance most of us would not have gotten. Hopefully, but probably not. The word came to him again, word for word the same as the first time, and this time he went (Jonah 3:3). Gotta give the man his due for that. He got up off that beach and walked toward the city he hated more than any place on earth. You have to understand what he was walking into. The Assyrians carved what they did to prisoners into their palace walls on purpose, so everyone who came through would read it and be afraid. They flayed people alive, impaled them, and stacked heads at the city gate. That was their advertising. Like I said Sunday, imagine being a Jewish man in 1945 and being sent to Berlin to preach mercy! And before any of this, Jonah had bought passage to Tarshish, the last dot on the edge of the map he knew. Nobody liquidates his life for a boat ticket unless the thing he is running from is costing him more than the money.

So he obeyed. Then look at what came out of his mouth when he got there. "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown" (Jonah 3:4). In Hebrew that is 5 words. There is no name of God in it and no offer of mercy anywhere. He had the whole Torah in his hands and he handed that city a wall where he could have handed them a door. He gave them the bare minimum he could give and still say he did what he was told, because he did not want it to work.

It worked anyway. The whole city turned, the king came down off his throne and sat in ashes, and God relented. That is the most effective sermon in the Old Testament (120000 repented), and it was preached by a man who was hoping it would fail!!

So hear this today, God can use you and never have you. Lemme say it again, "God can use you and never have you."

If you measure yourself by results, you will look at the fruit on the outside of your life and assume something happened on the inside. Church Attendance, check. You're on the schedule. You gave and the city repented. None of that proves one thing about what is going on inside you.

Paul told the Galatians they were called to freedom, and that the way they serve one another is through love (Galatians 5:13). Love is driving that verse, not obligation. Jonah walked into Nineveh with a contract in his pocket and completely forgot that Israel was in a covenant.  The same covenant Jonah called on when he was at the bottom of the sea.  You and I are a covenant people, not a people under contract. But God still has a people to love and a world to reach. Even if He's using you, it doesn't mean you've practiced total surrender.

**"God can use you and never have you."**

## Application

1. Where in your life are you doing the right thing while your heart stays somewhere else entirely?
2. What is one thing you could do this week only because you want to, with nobody keeping score?

## Challenge

Pick one thing you did for God this week and ask yourself honestly what moved you to do it. Write down the real answer, not the one you would give out loud in a small group. Keep that piece of paper somewhere you can find it, because you are going to need it again on Friday.

## Prayer

Father, I have been counting up what I do for You and calling it closeness to You. Show me today where my obedience is a payment instead of a response. I do not want to walk into my own Nineveh with a contract in my pocket and my heart somewhere else. Teach me what it is to serve because I love You and not because I owe You. In Jesus' name, amen.
