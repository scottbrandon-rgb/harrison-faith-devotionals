## Devotional

Sunday morning I went to pull up my notes and they were gone. They were there, and then they were not there, and I am standing in front of a room full of people telling myself don't panic, don't panic, don't panic, it's all good. Some of you watched me do it, but it's in those testing moments that what comes out is what has been put in.  That's what happened with me and that's what happened with Jonah.

Jonah points us to a major theme in this whole book, and he does it before he has any idea he is talking about himself. "Those who pay regard to vain idols forsake their hope of steadfast love" (Jonah 2:8).

Look hard at that word forsake. In Hebrew it carries the sense of letting go, turning loose, or abandoning something you had been holding onto. Read the verse again and see what it is actually telling you. Nobody took God's love away from Jonah. God never withdrew it, never shut it off, never held it back over a disappointment. Steadfast love is something you and I turn loose of, on purpose, so we can get a hand around something else and it's dangerous.

And this is the part that gets me. Steadfast love is the exact phrase Jonah throws at God in chapter 4. He preached the verse in the belly of the fish and then he became the verse outside the city wall. His hands were open down there because he had nothing left to hold, and the minute he was back on dry ground and no longer in need, the fingers started closing again.

Watch what God does about it. Jonah goes out east of the city, builds himself a little shelter out of sticks, and sits down in the heat hoping to watch fire fall on 120,000 people. God appoints a plant to come up over his head, and "Jonah was exceedingly glad because of the plant" (Jonah 4:6). The Hebrew doubles that word. He rejoiced with great rejoicing. Church, that's the only joy in the entire book. 4 chapters with a storm, a rescue, a citywide revival, and the one time this man is happy is over a shrub! A city gets saved and he's furious. He gets a little shade and it's the best day of his life.

Jeremiah paints the picture for us, "My people have committed two evils: they have forsaken me, the fountain of living waters, and hewed out cisterns for themselves, broken cisterns that can hold no water" (Jeremiah 2:13). There is that word again. Forsaken. Turned loose of. God never shut the water off they just walked away from the spring, took a chisel, cut a tank out of the rock to their own design, and it started leaking.

So you can attend and serve and give and sing and teach and tithe and all the things and still run dry. The spring is running exactly the way it always has. The crack is in the thing you built to catch it.

So when I stood up Sunday with empty hands and realized God did not need my notes. I also realized He's never once needed anything I was holding on to.

**Open your hand today, before God has to empty it for you.**

## Application

1. What is the one thing you are holding right now that you would argue with God to keep?
2. What would it look like to turn loose of that thing this week, in a way somebody else could actually see?

## Challenge

Hold your hands open in front of you for 60 seconds today and say out loud what is in them. Do it somewhere private if you need to, but do it. The posture will tell you things your prayers have been talking around for months.

## Prayer

Father, I have been holding something so tightly that I cannot receive anything else from You. Show me what it is and give me the honesty to say it out loud. I turned loose of Your steadfast love to keep my grip on it, and I want Your love back. Pry my fingers open if that's what it takes. In Jesus' name, amen.
