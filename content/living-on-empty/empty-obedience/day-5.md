## Devotional

We are still in awe around here. VBS week ended and our people cannot stop talking about it, about what God did in those kids and in the volunteers who kept showing up every night. I have watched grown adults get emotional trying to describe a room full of children.

Hold onto that, and then read how this book ends.

"And the LORD said, 'You pity the plant, for which you did not labor, nor did you make it grow, which came into being in a night and perished in a night. And should not I pity Nineveh, that great city, in which there are more than 120,000 persons who do not know their right hand from their left, and also much cattle?'" (Jonah 4:10-11)

God takes a plant and a city, sets them on the same scale, and lets Jonah watch which way his own heart tips.

Now look at how God describes the people He is defending. 120,000 who do not know their right hand from their left. Many read that as children. Others read it as an entire city with the moral awareness of a child, people who honestly could not have told you which way was up. Either way, God's closing argument for mercy is that they are small and they do not know any better.

So put the 2 rooms side by side. Our church spent a week in a building full of children who do not know their right hand from their left, and we came out of it undone by what God did in there. Jonah spent a week outside a city full of the same kind of people, and he wanted to die because God spared them. That is the whole distance this book is measuring.

And then it just stops. God asks the question and Jonah never answers it. There is no last verse where the prophet repents, no scene where he comes down off that hill and walks into the city rejoicing. The book ends with a question hanging in the air, waiting on whoever happens to be reading it.

Which means it is your turn to answer it.

Who is on your list? How long has that name been sitting there? What are you holding so tightly right now that you could not have celebrated what we celebrated this week if God had done it for somebody you already wrote off?

I told you Sunday that the tank runs empty. Because you cannot be filled with a closed hand.

Think about this, God is delighted in giving mercy to somebody you gave up on. He has been delighted in their response the whole time. And He is asking you the same question He asked Jonah, and He is waiting just as patiently for an answer.

**God no longer assigns evil to people who have recieved mercy. Have you?**

## Application

1. Who did you give up on, and what would it honestly cost you to watch God bless them?
2. What is one way you could stand with somebody this week who is still holding onto something they cannot get free of alone?

## Challenge

Think of the person you have written off and thank God for them out loud, by name, before the day is over. You will not mean it the first time you say it. Do it anyway, and then do it again tomorrow.

## Prayer

Father, You pitied a city that had earned nothing from You, and You have pitied me on those same terms. I have been furious about mercy landing on people I decided were finished. Give me the joy I watched in our church this week and let it reach all the way to the ones I wrote off. My hand is open. That is all I have today. In Jesus' name, amen.
