## Devotional

Elisha doesn't answer her. He asks her a question instead, and it's a strange one to put to a woman about to lose her children. What have you in the house? (2 Kings 4:2).

Before God does anything with her emptiness, He takes inventory of it.

Her answer comes in 2 pieces. Nothing in the house, and then, except a jar of oil.

That jar is smaller than you're picturing. The Hebrew word is asuk, and it shows up exactly once in the entire Old Testament, right here in this verse. It's a small flask, the kind kept for anointing oil, the sort of thing you'd use one time and set back down. When she says except this, she's holding up something that fits in her hand.

Pastor Caleb went after something Sunday that most of us are guilty of. Somebody asks how you're doing and you say "I'm good." Somebody asks about your week and you say "I'm good." Your marriage is 4 months into a silence neither of you will break, and you say "I'm good."

Pride will always coach you to perform wholeness when in reality you are empty.

Here's why that costs you something practical. When pride has the microphone, there's nothing in your hands for God to work with. He asks what's in the house and you tell Him everything's fine, so there's no jar to pour from, because you won't admit you're holding one. God isn't going to hand you instructions for a lack you won't admit you have.

Paul learned this from the other side of it. He asked God 3 times to take the thorn away, and the answer he got back was, "My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness" (2 Corinthians 12:9). Then Paul says the thing that sounds insane until you've lived it, that he'd rather glory in his weaknesses so the power of Christ would rest on him.

Now notice something about her honesty, because she told the truth about 2 things and not just 1.

She admitted the nothing. Plenty of us can do that part, because there's a kind of despair that's almost comfortable, where you say I've got nothing and you mean it as a way to quit trying. She didn't stop there. She said I've got nothing except, and that except is where every drop of oil came from.

You've got an except. It's small, it's a little embarrassing, and it isn't close to what this situation requires.

**Tell God the truth about how little you have, and don't leave out the little.**

## Application

1. Honestly, when is the last time you gave somebody the real answer when they asked how you were doing?
2. Who is one person you could tell this week what you're actually carrying?

## Challenge

Find the person God has already put close to you, the one you trust and have been handing the polished answer to anyway. Tell them one true thing about where you are this week. Not the whole story if you're not ready for that, just one sentence that's actually true.

## Prayer

Father, I've gotten good at saying I'm good. I've said it so many times I've nearly convinced myself, and the whole time You've been asking me what's actually in the house. So here it is. I'm emptier than I've let anybody see, and what I'm holding is a lot smaller than what this situation needs. I'm handing You the little bit anyway, because You've never needed much to work with.
