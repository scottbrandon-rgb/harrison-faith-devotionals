## Devotional

Hear the instruction like it's the first time. You're broke, the creditor is coming for your children, and the man of God sends you knocking on your neighbors' doors for empty containers. Bring back a lot of them, he says (2 Kings 4:3).

Then he tells her to go inside and shut the door (4:4).

That's the detail I keep coming back to. The miracle happened where nobody could watch it. Just a woman, her 2 boys, a closed door, and a flask that had no business lasting 3 pours.

Pastor Caleb called this being faithful with hidden obedience, and he told 2 stories on himself to get at it.

He was still a little sore about the first one. Their homeowner's insurance dropped them while the family was away on summer vacation, after promising they wouldn't. That left 8 or 9 days to find a new policy before the mortgage company picked one for them at a premium they couldn't cover. He called company after company. Denied, denied, denied, all of it over some old water claims. He said it took over his thinking, and he finally decided to quit calling and see what happened.

Then his wife sent him a screenshot. Home and auto, both marked paid. He asked her how she pulled it off, and that's when it hit him that she didn't do it and he didn't do it. The new policy came in cheaper than what they'd been paying, car insurance included.

Here's the part I appreciated him saying out loud from the platform. He told us he can't take any credit in that story, because he wasn't trusting God the way he should have been the entire time he was stressing over it.

The second story is his son. Brexton is about to turn 8, and he's learning to recognize the voice of God. He came home one afternoon, went straight to his room, and shut the door. A few minutes later he came out and Pastor Caleb asked what he'd been doing in there. He said the Lord told him to pray for some people and to do it right then, so he went to his room, closed the door, and did it.

He didn't announce it. He didn't go find his friends first. An 8 year old boy heard something and shut a door.

Same door as the widow's.

Jesus said it plainly. "One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much" (Luke 16:10). We all want the much. Pastor Caleb pointed out that nobody walks into a restaurant, dips 1 chip, and leaves. Everybody wants full. But kingdom fullness is not measured by how successful you are, it is measured by how faithful you are.

So what's the thing God asked you to do that nobody is ever going to see? The chairs you stack. The nursery shift instead of the platform. The mess you clean up that you didn't make.

**Do the thing today that nobody will ever see you do.**

## Application

1. What has God asked you to do that you've been putting off until somebody notices?
2. What is one small act of obedience you could finish today with the door shut?

## Challenge

Pick the assignment you've been treating as beneath you, the one with no audience attached to it, and do it today without telling anyone you did. If somebody happens to thank you for it, let that be the end of it and don't bring it up again.

## Prayer

Lord, I've been holding out for an assignment worth being seen doing. I've stepped over the small things You put right in front of me because there was no credit in them, and I called that discernment when it was really pride. Teach me to shut the door. Let me be as quick to obey You as an 8 year old boy who heard You and went straight to his room. I'll take the hidden thing today and leave what it becomes up to You.
