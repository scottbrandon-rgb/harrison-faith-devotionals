## Devotional

Her husband is dead. The debt he left behind is still owed, and the man holding that note is walking up the road to collect her 2 sons as payment (2 Kings 4:1). Here's the part that should bother you. The creditor wasn't breaking any law. Debt servitude was legal in Israel, and a man who couldn't pay could be worked until the debt cleared. On paper he had every right to be walking up that road.

But the law had fences around it, and the fences were built for people exactly like her. A widow was the test case for whether Israel actually believed what God said about the vulnerable. "You shall not mistreat any widow or fatherless child" (Exodus 22:22). Then comes the warning, that if they cry out to God, He will surely hear their cry (Exodus 22:23).

So the creditor was legal and he was still wrong. The letter permitted it. The covenant forbade it.

Now look at what she does. Your Bible says she cried to Elisha, and the Hebrew there is tsa'aq. It's a legal word. It's the sound a person makes when they're appealing to a court that outranks the one that just ruled against them. It's the same kind of cry Israel raised under Egyptian slavery, when "their cry for rescue from slavery came up to God" (Exodus 2:23), and God moved a whole nation over it.

She couldn't out argue the creditor. She had no money, no husband, and nothing to bargain with. So she went over his head. In those days a prophet stood as the Lord's representative, which means crying to Elisha was crying to God.

You've seen this before. A woman had bled for 12 years, spent everything she had on doctors, and only got worse. She didn't ask permission or wait for a decent moment. She told herself that if she could just touch His clothes she'd be made well, and she shoved through a crowd to do it. Jesus stopped walking and turned around (Mark 5:30).

Desperate faith turned Him around.

So here's the question Pastor Caleb put in front of us. What if the shift from living on empty to living on full isn't determined by your diligence but by your desperation? Your emptiness isn't proof God forgot you, it's room for Him to move.

You've called everybody you know. You've run the numbers again and gotten the same answer. You've worked every angle you have, and the creditor is still coming up the road.

You've got an appeal left.

**Quit arguing with the creditor and take it to the Judge.**

## Application

1. Where in your life have you been managing a situation instead of crying out to God about it?
2. What would it look like to pray today about the one thing you've already decided is too far gone?

## Challenge

Pick the situation you stopped praying about because you already decided how it ends. Say it out loud to God today, plainly, and ask Him for what you actually want instead of what you've talked yourself into settling for. Do it before you make another phone call about it.

## Prayer

Lord, I've worked this thing from every angle I know and I'm out of angles. I've called everybody who might help and read the same numbers 15 times, and none of it has moved an inch. So I'm bringing it to You as an appeal, because You're the only One who can overturn what's coming. You said You hear the widow when she cries out to You. I'm counting on that being true for me too.
