## Devotional

She's pouring and the jars are filling, so she tells her son to bring her another one. He tells her there isn't another one. That's when the oil stopped (2 Kings 4:6).

Nothing ran out in heaven that day. The flask never hit bottom. The oil quit flowing because the room ran out of empty things to receive it.

Pastor Caleb said it this way Sunday, that it wasn't a limit of God's supply, it was a limit of capacity. Her capacity wasn't determined by her circumstances. It wasn't determined by Elisha. It was determined by how far she was willing to go in obedience to prepare room for what she didn't yet have.

Now go back and read verse 3 with that in view. Empty vessels, and not too few. He told her the size of the miracle in advance and then handed her the pen. Every door she knocked on that afternoon was a decision about how much oil she'd end up with, and she had no way of knowing that while she was knocking.

Then look at how it ends, because the ending is bigger than the crisis required. "Go, sell the oil and pay your debts, and you and your sons can live on the rest" (2 Kings 4:7). The debt was the emergency. God cleared the debt and then funded the rest of their lives.

Let me tell you where this got real for us this weekend.

We brought in the Ability Tree partnership and trained 35 people for the Able Kids Ministry that launches in October. 35. And when the training was over, the table in the back stayed busy with people asking how they could be part of it.

Pastor Caleb said it from the platform before those numbers were in hand. We don't have the means for everything this ministry is going to need. That was true Sunday and it's still true this morning. So we're doing exactly what the widow did. We're knocking on doors and gathering empty vessels, and the oil is already running.

We need more buddies. 35 is a great start and it isn't the finish, because a disability should never be the thing that stops a child from hearing about Jesus.

Here's what I want you carrying into the weekend. The only thing that stopped the oil in that house was a shortage of empty vessels. God had more. He always had more.

Paul prayed toward a God "who is able to do far more abundantly than all that we ask or think" (Ephesians 3:20). Far more abundantly than we know how to ask for. So the question was never whether God had it.

Your emptiness isn't proof God forgot you, it's room for Him to move. She spent an afternoon collecting emptiness, and it fed her family for the rest of their lives.

Go get another vessel.

**Keep bringing Him empty things and the oil keeps coming.**

## Application

1. Where have you been praying for overflow while quietly making no room to receive it?
2. What is one step you could take this week to prepare room for something God hasn't given you yet?

## Challenge

Settle on one specific thing you're believing God for, then do something this week that only makes sense if He comes through. Make the call, clear the calendar, have the conversation, sign up for Able Kids. Prepare the room before the oil shows up.

## Prayer

Father, I've been asking You for more while I stood here holding out 1 small jar, hoping You'd fill it and calling that faith. You've never once been the limit in this. I've been the limit. So I'm going after more empty vessels and I'm going to keep bringing them until You tell me to stop. You're able to do far more than I know how to ask for, and I want somewhere to put it when it comes.
