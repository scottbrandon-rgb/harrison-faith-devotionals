## Devotional
Think about the people whose work you only notice when it goes wrong. The sound guy gets mentioned once a year, and it's the Sunday the mic squeals. The worship team rehearses on a Thursday night you'll never see. A nursery worker spends the whole service rocking your baby so you can hear the sermon, and maybe she gets a thank you on the way out, and maybe she doesn't. Each of them signed up to serve, and each of them still notices when a thank you never comes. Ingratitude is the quiet tax on everyone who serves.

Jesus finished his story with a question that stings. "Does he thank the servant because he did what was commanded? So you also, when you have done all that you were commanded, say, 'We are unworthy servants; we have only done what was our duty'" (Luke 17:9-10). The word unworthy is achreios. It describes a servant with no claim on his master, someone who has done the job and has nothing extra coming to him.

That sounds harsh until you remember who the Master is. Jesus "emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant," and "humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross" (Philippians 2:7-8). He came as a servant when he had every right to come as a King, and he washed feet that would run from him before sunrise. He died for you long before you were grateful, long before you had lived one considerate day toward his sacrifice. Once you see that, the word duty starts to change shape. Duty becomes devotion, something you long to give.

Ingratitude goes after your focus. When your eyes drift off Jesus, you start keeping track of what you're owed, who noticed it and who thanked you for it, until every Sunday starts to feel like an invoice. When your eyes stay on Jesus, you can't stop thinking about what he's paid. The same Lord who saw a safety cable at 3:30 in the morning sees every diaper you change and every cord you coil, and he records every bit of it.

Paul said to "count others more significant than yourselves" (Philippians 2:3), and you can only do that with your eyes fixed on the One who counted you worth a cross. A body only works when every part serves the others, and the Head of this body served first, served lowest, and served all the way to the grave. Every towel you pick up this week is a small echo of his.

**Keep your eyes on what he's paid, and you'll lose track of what you're owed.**

## Application
1. Where have you been keeping score of thank yous you never got?
2. Who is one person who serves behind the scenes that you could thank by name this week?

## Challenge
Before you serve again, take 5 minutes and read Philippians 2:5-11 out loud. Then find one person whose service usually goes unnoticed, like the sound tech or a nursery worker, and thank them specifically for what they do.

## Prayer
Jesus, you came as a servant when you deserved a throne, and you paid a debt I could never cover. Forgive me for the times I've kept count of what I was owed. Turn my duty into devotion and fix my eyes on what you've paid. Let every bit of my serving this week be a thank you back to you. Amen.
