## Devotional
When the angel of the Lord found Gideon, he was threshing wheat in a winepress (Judges 6:11). Threshing belonged on a hilltop where the wind could carry off the chaff, and a winepress was a pit carved into the rock. Gideon was beating grain in a hole in the ground so the Midianites wouldn't see him, and the angel greeted him with, "The LORD is with you, O mighty man of valor" (Judges 6:12).

Gideon's answer sounds like a lot of people sitting in our chairs on Sunday. "Behold, my clan is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house" (Judges 6:15). Yesterday we looked at the insecurity that thinks it's too big to serve. There's a second kind, and it may be the more common one in the church. It's the insecurity that thinks it's too small to serve.

Scripture is full of it. Moses told God, "I am slow of speech and of tongue," and then flat out asked, "please send someone else" (Exodus 4:10, 13). When Samuel came to anoint a king, Jesse lined up 7 sons and left David out in the field with the sheep, because no one in the family thought to call him (1 Samuel 16:11). On the day Israel's first king was presented, the Lord had to tell the crowd, "Behold, he has hidden himself among the baggage" (1 Samuel 10:22). The Hebrew word there means gear or equipment, so picture the future king of Israel crouched behind the suitcases.

Maybe that's you. You've seen the sign up for the nursery or the parking team and thought somebody better than you should take it. People haven't thought much of you, so you haven't thought much of yourself, and you've quietly decided you have nothing to offer the body. Paul wrote to the Corinthians, a church that loved ranking people, and told them God does his recruiting from the bottom of the list. "God chose what is low and despised in the world, even things that are not, to bring to nothing things that are, so that no human being might boast in the presence of God" (1 Corinthians 1:28-29).

Look at what God does with the nothings. He goes looking for the people who feel like nothing, and he uses them to bring to nothing the people who think they have to be somebody to be used. He does it on purpose, so that no one can stand in his presence and share the credit. The very sense that you have nothing to bring might be what makes you useful in his hands. Gideon's 300 men beat an army that covered the valley "like locusts in abundance" (Judges 7:12), and every person who heard that story knew exactly who won it.

**If you feel like nothing, you're exactly who God goes looking for.**

## Application
1. When have you talked yourself out of serving because you assumed someone else would do it better?
2. What is one place you could offer to serve this week, even if you feel like the least qualified person in the room?

## Challenge
Write down the reason you've given yourself for not serving, in one honest sentence. Then write 1 Corinthians 1:27 underneath it and read the two side by side. Before Sunday, tell one person at church where you'd like to help.

## Prayer
Father, you found Gideon in a winepress and Saul behind the baggage, and you knew right where they were. I confess I've hidden behind my own sense of not being enough. Thank you that you choose the weak things on purpose, so the glory stays with you. Pull me out of hiding and put me to work in your body. Amen.
