## Devotional
On the last night before the cross, the upper room had a problem. Dusty roads and open sandals meant every guest walked in with dirty feet, and in that culture washing them was the job of the lowest servant in the house. There was no servant in that room. There were 12 men who, that same night, argued about which of them was the greatest (Luke 22:24).

John tells us what Jesus knew before he moved. "Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he had come from God and was going back to God, rose from supper" (John 13:3-4). Look at how close John puts the greatness and the towel. Jesus knew he held all things, and his very next move was to tie a towel around his waist and kneel in front of the men who should have been serving him. He served because of who he was. His identity was so settled that a basin of dirty water had no power to shake it.

Secure people can't put down the towel, and insecure people won't pick it up. To be insecure is to be unsure and unsteady about who you are, so you avoid anything that might rattle that identity, and few things rattle an unsure identity like a job that feels beneath you.

Nehemiah's list of wall builders has one ugly line in it. "And next to them the Tekoites repaired, but their nobles would not stoop to serve their Lord" (Nehemiah 3:5). The Hebrew says they would not bring their necks to the work, the picture of an ox refusing the yoke. Meanwhile Nehemiah, the king's cupbearer and governor of the whole province, wrote, "I also persevered in the work on this wall" (Nehemiah 5:16). The man with the most status had mortar on his hands, and the men with less were too important to stoop.

Rehoboam got the same test and failed it in public. The old counselors told him, "If you will be a servant to this people today and serve them... then they will be your servants forever" (1 Kings 12:7). He refused, and 10 tribes walked away from him. Paul would later write that "the head" cannot say "to the feet, 'I have no need of you'" (1 Corinthians 12:21), and Rehoboam said it anyway with his choice. Insecurity that robs you of your function will rob you of your connection too. If you've been around this church a while and still feel like you're on the outside, the towel might be your way in.

**The towel never threatened Jesus, so it doesn't have to threaten you.**

## Application
1. Where is there a job at church or at home that you've quietly decided is beneath you?
2. What would it look like to pick up one towel this week that no one would expect you to touch?

## Challenge
Find one task this week that someone with your title or reputation usually skips, and do it without announcing it. Stack the chairs or take out the trash. Pay attention to what rises up in you while you do it, and bring that to God in prayer.

## Prayer
Jesus, you knew all things were in your hands, and you still knelt with a towel. Settle my identity in you so deeply that no task can threaten it. Show me where pride has kept me from serving and where insecurity has kept me from connecting. Make me secure enough to stoop. Amen.
