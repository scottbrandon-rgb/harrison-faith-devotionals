## Devotional
Have you ever been on a team where 20 percent of the people did 80 percent of the work? Maybe it was a school project where 2 kids carried the grade for 5, or a job where everybody was too busy to lend you a hand, or a marriage where one spouse is holding up the whole roof. Whatever the setting, the result is the same. When the few do the work of the many, the many drift and the few start keeping score.

The church in Corinth had its own version of that problem. They had ranked the gifts of the Spirit like a draft board, and the people with the impressive gifts treated everybody else like the bench. Paul answered them with a picture every Corinthian had already seen. Their city had a temple to Asclepius, the Greek god of healing, and worshipers who believed they had been healed left clay models of the body part that got better. Archaeologists have dug up hands, feet, eyes, and ears by the dozens. A Corinthian knew exactly what a body part looked like sitting on a shelf by itself, cut off from everything it used to do.

So Paul wrote, "For just as the body is one and has many members, and all the members of the body, though many, are one body, so it is with Christ" (1 Corinthians 12:12). The Greek word for member is melos, a limb. Paul means it the way a doctor would mean it, an arm or a kidney or a foot, a part with a job to do. Then he tells us why God built it that way. God gave "greater honor to the part that lacked it, that there may be no division in the body, but that the members may have the same care for one another" (12:24-25).

Watch the chain in that verse. Every member has honor, so every member is cared for, so every member must serve. "The same care for one another" means no one in the body lives only on the receiving end. I call the opposite ecclesiastical socialism, a church where a faithful few carry the load while everyone else collects the same benefits for free. History has watched that system fail in every government that tried it, and it fails in the church for the same reasons.

Your own body already proves Paul's point. When one part quits doing its job, you end up in a waiting room trying to figure out what went wrong. Paul says the church works the same way: "so we, though many, are one body in Christ, and individually members one of another" (Romans 12:5). You were made to be a working part of this body, and God never meant for you to sit on the shelf.

**If you're part of the body, you've got a job in it.**

## Application
1. Honestly, at church are you more often one of the few carrying the load or one of the many being carried?
2. What is one need at church you've noticed for a while that you could step in and meet this week?

## Challenge
Make a list of every place you receive something at church: a classroom someone set up, a song someone rehearsed, a coffee pot someone filled, a parking lot someone directed. Then make a second list of the places you give back. If the second list is short, pick one spot and ask about it before Sunday.

## Prayer
Father, you put this body together on purpose, and you did not make a single spare part. Thank you for the people who have carried loads I never noticed. Forgive me for the places I've let the few do the work of the many. Show me where I belong in this body and give me a heart that wants the job. Amen.
