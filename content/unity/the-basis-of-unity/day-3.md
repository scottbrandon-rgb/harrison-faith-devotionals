## Devotional
Picture the room for a second. In Corinth a household slave and the man who owned him could belong to the same church, and on the night they gathered they ate from the same loaf. In a Roman colony organized around the question of who outranked whom, that was socially unthinkable, and it created exactly the pressure you would expect it to create.

Corinth was a rebuilt city full of people on the way up. Freedmen who had bought their way out of slavery. Tradesmen with money and no pedigree. Jewish believers, Greek converts, and a handful of wealthy household heads who were accustomed to hosting and to being deferred to.

A Roman dinner seated its guests by rank and served better food to the better couches, and everyone in the room knew where they sat and why. Then these same people became one body and had to work out what to do with a table where rank carried no weight.

That is the room Paul writes into. "If the foot should say, 'Because I am not a hand, I do not belong to the body,' that would not make it any less a part of the body" (1 Corinthians 12:15). He comes at it from both ends. The member tempted to count himself out and the member tempted to count somebody else out get the same answer from the same apostle.

Then Paul makes the turn. "But God has so composed the body, giving greater honor to the part that lacked it, that there may be no division in the body, but that the members may have the same care for one another" (1 Corinthians 12:24-25). God composed it himself. He weighted the honor deliberately toward the members who had none of it, and he did it for a stated purpose, so the body would never tear along the seam where the world normally tears.

He says it to the Galatians in a single line. "There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is no male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus" (Galatians 3:28). Those were the three deepest divides in the ancient world and Paul does not soften one of them. He says they have lost their authority to decide who belongs.

Bring it into your week. Your politics, your family history, your income, the decade you were born in, and your opinion about the volume of the music all have exactly as much power to remove you from this body as being a Greek did in Corinth. That power adds up to nothing. And the person across the room you would never have picked was placed here on purpose, and probably carries the honor you happen to lack.

**God composed this body himself, and he put the honor you lack in somebody you would not have chosen.**

## Application
1. Who in this church have you quietly filed under too different to bother with?
2. What is one honor you could give this week to someone here who rarely receives any?

## Challenge
Think of the person in this church furthest from you in background, age, or politics, and find one thing about them you actually admire. Tell them this week, out loud and specifically. Skip the easy compliment about their outfit and go after something that cost them.

## Prayer
Father, you composed this body yourself and you gave the greater honor to the parts that lacked it, which means you have already settled things I keep reopening. Show me the seams where I have been quietly deciding who counts around here. Give me eyes for the member I have overlooked and the humility to receive what they carry. Let there be no division in this body while I have anything to do with it. Amen.
