## Devotional

I loved the fact that Greg stood up front Sunday morning and let me pour a pitcher of water over his head! I went with water instead of oil because I wasn't going to ruin the man, but the picture held anyway. Watch where it went. I didn't pour it down his back, and I didn't put it on his shoulders or his chest. It went on the head, and everything lined up underneath the head got wet.

David says it twice inside a single verse, running down the beard and running down the collar, so the direction is impossible to miss (Psalm 133:2). He doesn't anoint the shoulders. He doesn't anoint the feet. He anoints the head, and everything under the head catches the overflow.

David didn't reach for Aaron at random. Aaron was the high priest, and the high priest wore something on his chest that most of us read straight past. "So Aaron shall bear the names of the sons of Israel in the breastpiece of judgment on his heart, when he goes into the Holy Place, to bring them to regular remembrance before the LORD" (Exodus 28:29). Twelve stones, twelve tribes, every name in the nation carried over his heart, and those same twelve names split six and six across the two stones on his shoulders (Exodus 28:9-10). So when the oil came off the head, ran the length of the beard and hit that collar, it ran across every name in Israel. One man was anointed and a whole nation was covered.

Now David moves from oil to weather without changing his point. "It is like the dew of Hermon, which falls on the mountains of Zion" (Psalm 133:3). Mount Hermon is the high ground in the far north and it holds snow all year long. Warm air rises, meets that cold, and the whole region gets a dew heavy enough at night to land like a rainstorm. Zion sits to the south. David takes the highest place in the north and the most significant place in the south and says the blessing stretches the full length of the land. Whatever sits within reach of that flow gets touched by it, and that includes the city you live in, water is vital in Israel. In Egypt a man opened a channel off the Nile, moved a little water with his foot, and watered his whole field without ever looking up. God pulled them out of there and set them down in "a land of hills and valleys, which drinks water by the rain from heaven" (Deuteronomy 11:11). There was no Nile to tap and no channel to open. You look up or you don't eat. He was building a people whose posture was dependence, and then he used a mountain and a weather system to show them he could be trusted with the water. Unity is what kept the people of Israel faithful before God and allowed the rains to fall upon them. 

"Behold, how good and pleasant it is when brothers dwell in unity." There is where brothers dwell under the head.

You already know you need a blessing, and I'm not going to spend a Monday convincing you of it. What you haven't asked is what you're standing under while you ask. God isn't walking over to anoint your shoulder because your shoulder has had a hard month. There are things in your life this morning that aren't lined up under him, your money, your marriage, your Tuesday at work, and whatever is coming down will never reach them sitting where they sit.

**What things in your life are you asking God to bless when, in reality, they just need alignment?**

## Application
1. Where in your life have you been asking God to bless something you never actually put under him?
2. What is one area you could move back under Christ's authority this week?

## Challenge
Take five minutes today and say the areas out loud. Your marriage, your money, your work, your kids. Tell the Lord which one you've been asking him to bless while keeping it out from under him. Don't try to fix it today, just write it down somewhere you'll see it again on Friday.

## Prayer
Father, you commanded the blessing at a place instead of a feeling, and I've spent a long time asking for the overflow while standing outside of it. Show me what I've kept back. Show me the room in my house, the line in my budget, the part of my week that has never once been under your authority. I'm not asking you to move the blessing. I'm asking you to move me. Amen.
