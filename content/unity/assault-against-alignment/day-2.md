## Devotional
About forty years before Jesus sent this letter, Paul called the elders of Ephesus down to the harbor at Miletus and warned them. "I know that after my departure fierce wolves will come in among you, not sparing the flock" (Acts 20:29). They took him seriously. By the time John wrote Revelation, Ephesus was the gateway church of Asia Minor, and a traveling preacher who wanted a hearing in that region had to get past Ephesus first. They tested every man who claimed to be an apostle, and Jesus says they caught the frauds.

Then Jesus says the hard part. "But I have this against you, that you have abandoned the love you had at first" (Revelation 2:4). This is the same church Paul once thanked God for because he had heard of their "love toward all the saints" (Ephesians 1:15). One generation later they were famous for catching wolves, and somewhere along the way they stopped loving the sheep they were guarding. You can be right and divide at the same time.

Paul saw the same danger in Rome. The letter that warns us about those "who cause divisions and create obstacles" (Romans 16:17) had already corrected believers who were right about food and wrong about their brother. "For if your brother is grieved by what you eat, you are no longer walking in love" (Romans 14:15). The word for "hindrance" back in Romans 14:13 is the same Greek word Paul uses for "obstacles" in Romans 16:17. A believer holding a true conviction can set the same trap a false teacher sets, and all it takes is truth that has stopped loving. "This 'knowledge' puffs up, but love builds up" (1 Corinthians 8:1).

Ephesus went wrong at the root. Jesus said the whole law hangs on two commandments, love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, mind, and strength, and love your neighbor as yourself (Mark 12:30-31). That's vertical love and horizontal love, and the second one flows out of the first. When Ephesus abandoned their first love, they abandoned their second love right along with it. If we aren't loving God upward, how will we ever love people outward?

Maybe you're the one in your family who knows the verses, catches the error, and wins the argument. Paul says you could "understand all mysteries and all knowledge," and if you "have not love," you are nothing (1 Corinthians 13:2). In Jesus, truth and love never come apart, because he came "full of grace and truth" (John 1:14). Unity requires truth and love, and truth that stops loving turns into brutality that splits a church or a marriage right down the middle.

**Truth without love is brutality.**

## Application
1. Honestly, where have you cared more about being right than about loving the person across from you?
2. Who is one person you've corrected more than you've loved, and what would it look like to build them up this week?

## Challenge
Think about the last argument you won. Reach out to that person today, leave the correction out of it, and tell them one thing you genuinely appreciate about them. Then read 1 Corinthians 13:1-7 with your own name everywhere Paul says love, and pay attention to where it stops sounding true.

## Prayer
Lord Jesus, you walk among the lampstands, and you see what we do right and what we've left behind. I don't want to be right and still be wrong. Give me back the love I had at first, for you and for the people you died for. Let the truth I know come out of me the way it came out of you, full of grace and truth. In Jesus' name, amen.
