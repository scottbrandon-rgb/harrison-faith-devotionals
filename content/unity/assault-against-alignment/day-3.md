## Devotional
Thyatira was a working town. Lydia, the seller of purple goods who opened her home to Paul, came from there (Acts 16:14), and the city was known for its trade guilds, dyers and potters and bronze workers and leather workers. If you wanted to work, you belonged to a guild, and the guilds held banquets for their patron gods with meat sacrificed to idols on the table and immorality close behind. Most historians think that's the door Jezebel walked through. She gave believers a spiritual reason to keep their seat at the table, and the church let her keep teaching.

Look at what Jesus commends first. "I know your works, your love and faith and service and patient endurance, and that your latter works exceed the first" (Revelation 2:19). Ephesus was losing its love while Thyatira was growing in it. Then the charge lands. "But I have this against you, that you tolerate that woman Jezebel" (Revelation 2:20). The word tolerate means to let something alone, to leave it be. Thyatira had real love, and somewhere along the way that love decided the kindest thing it could do was say nothing.

That's where a lot of the church sits right now. We know the truth, and we've let the culture tell us where to be quiet about it. The world has a word for the church it wants, and the word is tolerant. It calls our silence loving, and we've believed it, because none of us wants to ruffle feathers at work or at the dinner table. The trouble is that silence is acceptance, and when the church goes quiet about what God has said, the world hears agreement.

We serve a speaking God. The first thing he did in creation was talk, "And God said, 'Let there be light'" (Genesis 1:3), and he has been speaking ever since. Salvation itself comes out of the mouth. "If you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved" (Romans 10:9). God's people are called to speak the truth and defend it, because love without truth deceives. It happens two ways, through a deceiver's counterfeit love and through a church's real love that won't confront. Thyatira had the second kind, and people still got hurt.

It doesn't stay at church either. It's the husband who knows his marriage is drifting and says nothing because he doesn't want the fight. It's the parent who watches a child walk toward something that will hurt them because correction feels unloving. It's the employee who sees the numbers fudged and keeps his head down to keep his job. Unity requires truth and love, and Thyatira's kind of love, the kind that goes quiet, is the kind Jesus held against them.

**Love that won't speak the truth has already agreed with the lie.**

## Application
1. Where has the culture taught you to go quiet about something God has clearly spoken on?
2. What is one truth you need to say out loud this week, in love, to someone you've been leaving alone?

## Challenge
Read Revelation 2:18-29 and underline what Jesus tells the faithful in Thyatira in verse 25, "hold fast what you have until I come." Then pick one conversation you've been avoiding because it might ruffle feathers, pray over it, and set a day this week to have it. Go in with love, and go in with the truth.

## Prayer
Father, you are a speaking God, and you did not stay silent when I was lost in my sin. Forgive me for every time I let the world call my silence love. Give me the courage to open my mouth and confess what you have said, and give me the love to say it in a way that can be received. Don't let me tolerate what you have called sin in my home, in my church, or in my own heart. In Jesus' name, amen.
