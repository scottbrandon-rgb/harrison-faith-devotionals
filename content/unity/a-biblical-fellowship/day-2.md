## Devotional
On paper, the church in Corinth had everything a church could want. They had spiritual gifts, gifted teachers, money, and a crowd. They also had factions lining up behind their favorite preachers, members suing each other, and a communion meal where some went home full and others went home hungry (1 Corinthians 11:21).

Before Paul corrects any of it, he reminds them how they got in. "God is faithful, by whom you were called into the fellowship of his Son, Jesus Christ our Lord" (1 Corinthians 1:9). That is the first attribute of biblical fellowship, a relationship with Jesus. God did the calling, and the relationship is what qualifies you. Your track record at church had nothing to do with getting you in the door.

The second attribute is inclusion into the body of Christ. "The cup of blessing that we bless, is it not a participation in the blood of Christ? The bread that we break, is it not a participation in the body of Christ?" (1 Corinthians 10:16). The word for participation there is koinonia, the same word Luke used for fellowship in Acts 2:42. Every time you take the bread and the cup, you are saying you share in his body, and his body includes the people sitting in your row.

This is where a lot of church people get uncomfortable. A skin tag is attached to the body and gets carried everywhere the body goes, and it contributes absolutely nothing, included and never participating. You can't be a skin tag in the body of Christ. Two chapters later Paul explains that God arranged every member in the body, each one of them, on purpose (1 Corinthians 12:18).

The third attribute is a shared faith. Paul wrote to his friend Philemon, "I pray that the sharing of your faith may become effective for the full knowledge of every good thing that is in us for the sake of Christ" (Philemon 6). Sharing is koinonia again. Paul was about to put that shared faith to the test, because he was sending back Onesimus, Philemon's runaway slave, who had come to Christ and was now "a beloved brother" (Philemon 16).

Put those 3 together and you can see why a church built on them survives the sieve. A relationship with Jesus brings you in, participation connects you to the body, and a shared faith gets worked out with real people, sometimes with the very person you would rather avoid. When the shaking comes, the skin tags are the first thing to fall off.

**Show up to participate, because the body of Christ has no room for skin tags.**

## Application
1. Where have you been attached to this church without really participating in it?
2. What is one way you could put your faith to work alongside another believer this week?

## Challenge
Pick one area of this church where you have been a spectator and ask someone this week how you can help. Walk into your life group ready to share something real about your week. The next time you take communion, look down your row and remember that those people are part of what you are receiving.

## Prayer
Father, you are faithful, and you called me into the fellowship of your Son when I had nothing to offer you. Forgive me for the seasons I have been attached to your body without participating in it. Show me where you want me to function. Give me the nerve to share my faith with the people you put beside me, especially the ones I would rather avoid. Amen.
