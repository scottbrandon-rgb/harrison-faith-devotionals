## Devotional
Picture a group of hikers on the same mountain. One is a rookie and one has climbed for 20 years, one packed light and one is hauling half his garage, and they are all aiming for different altitudes. The one thing every one of them trusts is a compass, and a compass points north no matter who is holding it.

That is the 7th attribute of biblical fellowship. It is objective, which means believers come together under a single, unchanging attribute that takes precedence over every other condition or characteristic. John tells you what it is: "our fellowship is with the Father and with his Son Jesus Christ" (1 John 1:3). The New English Bible puts it this way, "that you and we together may share in a common life, that life which we share with the Father and his Son Jesus Christ." That common life is our north.

Paul says "we, though many, are one body in Christ, and individually members one of another" (Romans 12:5). So your feelings don't get to decide your place in the body. How you feel about politics and presidents, women's hormones, immigration, abortion, same sex marriage, ICE, men in women's sports, or race does not change your relationship to the body. The truth that offends you is not enough to remove you.

Your interests don't decide it either, and that includes your hometown pride. Your activity doesn't decide it. Your devotional life, prayer life, attendance, serving, and giving should be the evidence of your relationship to the body, and evidence grows out of a relationship that already exists. Your experiences don't decide it, even if someone in this church did you wrong (you're not the first), even if the pastor said something that hurt you, even if God didn't show up the way you hoped he would.

Early in our marriage, Julie and I disagreed about a lot, more than I'd like to admit. What carried us was love, and even more than love, covenant. We shared a goal, a healthy marriage, and the covenant kept us at the table long enough to work out everything else.

The body works the same way, and so do the churches across this city. We will have different opinions and real disagreements, and we are still bound together in the covenant blood of Jesus Christ. Because we share one life, we can work through our differences and stay one body. When the sifting comes, the compass still points north.

**Disagree when you have to, and keep the life you share in Christ as your north.**

## Application
1. Which of your feelings or experiences have you let decide how connected you are to this church?
2. Who is one believer you strongly disagree with that you could pray for by name this week?

## Challenge
Think of the believer you disagree with most, whether it's about politics, a social issue, or something that happened at church. Pray for that person by name every day this week. Before Sunday, find a way to tell them you're glad they're in the body.

## Prayer
Father, thank you that my fellowship is with you and with your Son, and that you have given me a common life with every believer. Forgive me for letting my feelings, my interests, and my hurts decide who I stay connected to. Hold this body together in covenant when we disagree. Keep your Son as the north every one of us trusts, and carry us through the sifting as one. Amen.
