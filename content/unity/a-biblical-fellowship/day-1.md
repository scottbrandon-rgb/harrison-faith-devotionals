## Devotional
Every farmer in Jesus' day knew what happened to wheat after the threshing floor. The grain went into a sieve, and somebody shook it hard, over and over, until the chaff and the dirt fell through and only the wheat stayed in the basket. The shaking was the whole point of the sieve.

That is the picture Jesus handed Peter the night before the cross. "Simon, Simon, behold, Satan demanded to have you, that he might sift you like wheat, but I have prayed for you that your faith may not fail" (Luke 22:31-32). Jesus let the sifting come, he prayed Peter through it, and then he gave him an assignment for the other side: "when you have turned again, strengthen your brothers." Sifting is miserable and productive at the same time.

Sunday I told you the Lord told me this church is going to go through a sifting season. I don't know when, but I know what he said will carry us through it, and that is unity. Last Monday we read Jesus' warning that "no city or house divided against itself will stand" (Matthew 12:25). A divided church gets sifted and scattered, while a unified church gets sifted and keeps its wheat.

Unity needs something solid underneath it, and most of what the world calls fellowship won't survive a good shake. Webster defines fellowship as "a community of interest, activity, feeling, or experience." Think about the groups built that way. An activist collective lasts until the cause is won, a support group lasts until the hurt heals, a team lasts until the season ends, and your online community lasts right up until you stop paying the internet bill. Each one gathers around something temporary, so each one fades when that thing does.

Luke describes something sturdier in the first church. "And they devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers" (Acts 2:42). The Greek word for fellowship is koinonia, and the verse right before it tells you who was in it. "So those who received his word were baptized" (Acts 2:41). To receive his word meant to accept what Peter preached and answer it with belief, and the opposite of receiving is rejecting.

That makes biblical fellowship exclusive. You can attend church, sit near it, watch it, even enjoy it, and still stand outside of it. In a culture where everyone expects a seat at every table, that may sound harsh. Biblical fellowship is no more exclusive than heaven itself, and the door into both is the same Jesus.

**Sifting is coming, so make sure what holds you to this church can survive the sieve.**

## Application
1. If this church got shaken hard tomorrow, what would actually still be holding you here?
2. Who is one person you could strengthen this week, before the shaking ever starts?

## Challenge
Write down the 3 or 4 things that keep you connected to this church, and be honest about each one. Circle anything that is really a preference, a program, or a person. Then pray over that list and ask God to make the life you share in Christ the reason you stay.

## Prayer
Father, you told Peter the sifting was coming, and you prayed for him before it ever started. Thank you that Jesus still prays for his people. Show me what I have been standing on that will fall through the sieve. Keep this church together when the shaking comes, and make me someone who strengthens my brothers on the other side of it. Amen.
