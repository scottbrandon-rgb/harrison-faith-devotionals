## Devotional
Luke slips a business detail into the story of Peter's big catch. James and John "were partners with Simon" (Luke 5:10). The Greek word is koinonos, from the same family as koinonia, and in a fishing trade it meant something specific. Partners shared the boat, the nets, the risk, the labor, and the profit. When the nets filled up, everybody pulled.

Paul carries that partnership language somewhere no fisherman would have signed up for. He wanted to "know him and the power of his resurrection, and ... share his sufferings, becoming like him in his death" (Philippians 3:10). That is the fourth attribute of biblical fellowship, a sharing of his suffering, and Paul wrote those words in chains (Philippians 1:13). When the sifting comes to this church, you go through it as a partner in his sufferings, and partners carry the loss together. Peter learned that the hard way after his own sifting, and later he wrote, "rejoice insofar as you share Christ's sufferings" (1 Peter 4:13). The word for share there comes from the same koinonia family.

The fifth attribute is contribution to the needy. Paul spent years collecting an offering from Gentile churches for poor believers in Jerusalem, people most of those givers would never meet. Macedonia and Achaia "have been pleased to make some contribution for the poor among the saints at Jerusalem" (Romans 15:26). He told the Corinthians their gift would cause others to "glorify God because of your submission that comes from your confession of the gospel of Christ, and the generosity of your contribution for them and for all others" (2 Corinthians 9:13). Contribution there is koinonia. Greeks who once had nothing in common with Jews were sending them money, because now they shared one life.

The sixth attribute is partnership in the gospel. Paul thanked the Philippians "because of your partnership in the gospel from the first day until now" (Philippians 1:5). That church started when a businesswoman named Lydia opened her home to Paul (Acts 16:14-15), and it kept sending him support when no other church did (Philippians 4:15). They were in the boat with him, pulling on the nets from hundreds of miles away.

Add those 3 up and you see what this partnership costs. You share his suffering, you share your resources, and you share the mission. A fellowship that splits the risk, the labor, and the profit can be shaken without being scattered, because every partner has something in the boat.

**Get in the boat and pull, because partners carry the load together.**

## Application
1. Honestly, do you relate to this church more like a partner or more like a customer?
2. What is one need in this church you could put your time or your money toward this week?

## Challenge
Find one practical need in this church this week and meet it, whether that's a family who needs a meal, a ministry short on volunteers, or a missionary we support. Skip waiting for an announcement from the stage. Then pray for that person or ministry by name every day through Friday.

## Prayer
Lord Jesus, you let me share in your life, and I am learning that includes sharing in your sufferings. Thank you for putting me in the boat with your people. Make me generous with the needy and faithful in the work of the gospel. When this church gets shaken, may I be found pulling on the nets beside my brothers and sisters. Amen.
