## Devotional
This week our life groups met for the first time, and some of you sat across from people you had only ever waved at from the other side of the sanctuary. Maybe it felt awkward, and maybe you drove home wondering if you'd go back. That awkward circle of chairs is where the one another commands of Scripture actually get obeyed. A life group is where you find out who needs a meal, who needs a phone call, and who has been faking fine for a month.

The key to biblical fellowship runs opposite of how most of us shop for a church. Biblical fellowship is about what you contribute, and in contributing, you receive what you need.

The apostles spelled out what that contribution looks like. "Love one another with brotherly affection. Outdo one another in showing honor" (Romans 12:10). Live in harmony with one another (Romans 12:16). Serve one another with your gifts (1 Peter 4:10), and show hospitality to one another without grumbling (1 Peter 4:9). Confess your sins to one another and pray for one another (James 5:16). Exhort one another every day (Hebrews 3:13). Do good to one another (1 Thessalonians 5:15), and encourage one another and build one another up (1 Thessalonians 5:11). Teach and admonish one another (Colossians 3:16), submit to one another (Ephesians 5:21), and bear one another's burdens (Galatians 6:2). Comfort one another, agree with one another, and greet one another (2 Corinthians 13:11-12).

The Greek word behind all of those is allelon, a word that only works in both directions. Every one of those commands needs a second person in the room. You can't bear a burden by yourself, and you can't confess your sins to a mirror.

Go back to where this week started. Jesus told Peter the sifting was coming and prayed for him, and then he said, "when you have turned again, strengthen your brothers" (Luke 22:32). Peter's assignment after the worst night of his life was a one another, and he did it. About 7 weeks later he stood up at Pentecost and preached the sermon that added 3,000 souls to the fellowship we started with on Monday (Acts 2:41). The same Jesus has prayed for you, called you into his fellowship, and given you a common life with the Father. Everything you contribute to this body, you first received from him.

Where there's unity, there's community, and a unified body is a life giving body. That is the kind of church that can be sifted without being scattered.

**Give yourself to the one anothers, because that is where you receive what you need.**

## Application
1. When have you walked into church asking only what it could do for you?
2. Which one another command could you practice with someone from your life group before Sunday?

## Challenge
Pick one of the one another commands from today's reading and put it into practice with someone from your life group before Sunday. Send the text, make the call, confess the thing, or carry the burden. If you missed the first meeting, call your leader today and ask where to show up next week.

## Prayer
Lord Jesus, you prayed for Peter before he was sifted, and you have prayed for me. Thank you for the common life I share with the Father, with you, and with this church. Make me a contributor in this body, quick to honor and quick to strengthen my brothers and sisters. Keep this church unified through whatever season is coming, so that when we are shaken we are still one. Amen.
