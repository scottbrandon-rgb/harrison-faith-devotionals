## Devotional
Every wedding has that moment. The minister looks out at the room and says, "If anyone present knows of any reason that this couple should not be joined in holy matrimony, speak now or forever hold your peace." Those words come to us from the Book of Common Prayer in 1549. Nearly 500 years later the room still goes quiet at that line, and everybody understands the silence means yes. The law calls this the Rule of Tacit Admission, and Black's Law Dictionary defines it as "an admission reasonably inferable from... a party's failure to act or speak."

Our legal system is built on that rule. If a squatter moves onto your land and you never evict him or charge him rent, your silence can hand him the property. If someone copies your trademark and you do nothing to defend it, you can lose your right to it. It's even written into the Constitution. Article I, Section VII says that if the President doesn't return a bill within 10 days, "the same shall be a Law, in like manner as if he had signed it." The President's silence equals his signature.

God wrote that principle into His law long before any court did. In Numbers 30 a young woman still living in her father's house makes a vow to the LORD. When "her father hears of her vow... and says nothing to her, then all her vows shall stand" (Numbers 30:4). The Hebrew word for says nothing is charash, to be silent, and older English Bibles translate it "hold his peace," the same phrase we still hear at weddings. If he opposes her "on the day that he hears of it," then "no vow of hers" shall stand (30:5). A father who heard and said nothing has agreed, and God holds him to it.

Leviticus takes the principle a step further. When a man in Israel gave one of his children to Molech, the god who demanded children, God said that if the people of the land "do at all close their eyes to that man... then I will set my face against that man and against his clan" (Leviticus 20:4-5). The Amplified Bible says they were "winking at his sin." God counted the ones who looked away right along with the one who did it, and the ballot still asks questions about the life of a child.

Here's what that means for you. When God's people say nothing, our silence signs what we know is wrong. A father in Israel had one day to speak up, and once that day passed the Word was settled, "all her vows shall stand."

**Whatever you hear and say nothing to, you've signed.**

## Application
1. Where in your life have you heard something you knew was wrong and said nothing because speaking up would cost you?
2. What is one conversation this week where you need to speak up so your silence doesn't sign for you?

## Challenge
Before you go to bed tonight, write down one thing you've stayed quiet about that Scripture clearly speaks to. Find the verse and write it beside it. Then ask the Lord whether this is your "day that he hears of it," the day to speak before your silence becomes a signature.

## Prayer
Lord, you heard every word I never said. You told the fathers of Israel that saying nothing was saying yes, and I know that word was written for me too. Forgive me for the times my silence agreed with something you call sin. Give me the courage to speak on the day I hear it, while speaking can still make a difference. Amen.
