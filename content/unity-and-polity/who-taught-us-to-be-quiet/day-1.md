## Devotional
November 3rd is Election Day. Between now and then we have 3 Sundays, and on those Sundays we're going to open the Word on the subjects a lot of people would rather their pastor leave alone. Here in Harrison we're electing a brand new mayor, and you can't sit down for a cup of coffee in this town without hearing somebody's take on it. Across the country, the questions in front of voters keep circling back to marriage, children, life in the womb, the border, policing and schools. This week of devotionals asks one question, who taught us to be quiet, and it's here to get your heart ready before those Sundays come, so that when we talk about government you already know why God's people can't sit this one out.

Why should a Christian care what happens on a Tuesday in November? Solomon answered that about 3,000 years ago. "What has been is what will be, and what has been done is what will be done, and there is nothing new under the sun" (Ecclesiastes 1:9). Social policy, immigration, health care, crime, foreign affairs and education are all old ground in Scripture. Problems only change their verbiage, people never change, and principles remain the same. That's why we say we follow One Book, and it's why you've heard me say, "Never put a problem before a person, and never put a person before a principle." A president is a principle, and so is a mayor.

Hear me on this, the people of God are the only qualified persons to speak on these matters of government. If God has already spoken to every one of those questions, then the people who carry His Word are the ones equipped to answer them. Paul told Timothy that Scripture is "profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work" (2 Timothy 3:16-17). That's exactly why the church gets both told and taught to be quiet. The world tells us to keep politics out of the pulpit, and too many pulpits have taught their people to agree.

Peter and John heard the same order 2,000 years ago. After God healed a lame man through them at the temple, the council "charged them not to speak or teach at all in the name of Jesus." They answered, "Whether it is right in the sight of God to listen to you rather than to God, you must judge, for we cannot but speak of what we have seen and heard" (Acts 4:18-20). In the Greek that last line reads, "we are not able not to speak." The double negative is the point, because silence was never on the table for them. Luke says the council saw their boldness, the Greek word parrēsia, which literally means all speech. In ancient Athens it was the right of a free citizen to stand up in the assembly and say everything on his mind. The council watched 2 uneducated fishermen speak with that kind of freedom, "and they recognized that they had been with Jesus" (Acts 4:13).

Somebody will say, "Pastor, Peter and John were preaching the Gospel, and you're talking about government." What is the Gospel? It's the life, death and resurrection of Jesus Christ, and everything that pertains to Him. On the road to Emmaus, "beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself" (Luke 24:27). The same Scriptures that point to Jesus speak to marriage, to life, to justice and to the people who govern. When the council dragged the apostles back in, Peter said it plainly, "We must obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29). So if we are to obey God rather than men, why are we so quiet?

**If you've been with Jesus, you're qualified to speak.**

## Application
1. Honestly, when the talk around town turns to the election, do you speak from Scripture or do you go quiet?
2. What would it look like to sit down with your ballot this year and an open Bible right beside it?

## Challenge
Find your sample ballot for November 3rd today and read every race and question on it, the mayor's race included. Beside each one, write down a passage of Scripture you believe speaks to it. Keep that list in your Bible, because we're going to need it on Sunday.

## Prayer
Father, you have spoken to every question my town and my country are arguing about, and you never asked me to keep your Word to myself. Forgive me for the times I've gone quiet because quiet was easier. Give me the boldness Peter and John had, the kind people could only explain by saying they had been with Jesus. As November 3rd gets closer, keep me in your Word so that when I speak, I'm saying what you've already said. Amen.
