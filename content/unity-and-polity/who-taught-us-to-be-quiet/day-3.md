## Devotional
Who taught us to be quiet? The lesson started in Europe after the Reformation, when the Peace of Augsburg in 1555 put the old rule of kings into a phrase, "whose is the reign, his is the religion." Whatever the king believed, his kingdom had to believe. People were left with religion without relationship, so the Pietists rose up and said you need a personal relationship with Jesus on top of good doctrine. They were right about that. They also taught that a holy person stays out of the bars, the theaters and the government. That became the German concept of the Two Kingdoms, the Kingdom of the Church and the Kingdom of the Government, and the two don't touch. That idea was the backbone of the separation of church and state, and 4 centuries of that teaching led to the rise of Hitler and the Holocaust of the Jews.

Pietism created a prophecy that fulfilled itself. Don't get involved in government because it's ungodly. Why is it ungodly? Because the godly aren't involved. Noninvolvement in America came from 18th century German Pietists. The 17th century Puritans built the opposite, a covenant that required everyone to take part in both church and state, and our government came from them.

Henry Muhlenberg was the founder of the Lutheran Church in America, and he had 2 sons who were Pietist Lutheran pastors. On March 23, 1775, John Peter Muhlenberg heard Patrick Henry's "Give me liberty or give me death" speech, went to General George Washington and offered to help. Washington made him a colonel and told him to go get his men. On January 21, 1776, at 30 years old, Pastor Muhlenberg preached from Ecclesiastes 3:1 and told his church, "In the language of the Holy Writ, there is a time for all things. There is a time to preach and a time to fight. And now is the time to fight." Then he took off his black clerical robe to reveal his officer's uniform in the Continental Army. He gave an altar call that day and 162 men came forward. The 8th Virginia Regiment was formed that day, 300 men in all, and they marched out of the church with drums leading the way. After fighting in many of the battles of the Revolution, he became a U.S. Senator in the first session of the U.S. Congress.

His brother Frederick, pastor of Christ Lutheran Church in New York City, wrote him, "You have become too involved in matters which, as a preacher, you have nothing whatsoever to do." He accused his brother of serving two masters. Peter wrote back, "Quit being a Tory British sympathizer." Later the British invaded New York, burned down Frederick's church and made his wife and children flee. Frederick's attitude changed, and he was elected to Congress and became the first Speaker of the U.S. House of Representatives. The Bill of Rights sent to the states for ratification carries 2 signatures, Vice President John Adams and Speaker Frederick Augustus Muhlenberg. Does anybody honestly think these 2 pastors would vote to outlaw themselves?

Solomon wrote that there is "a time to keep silence, and a time to speak" (Ecclesiastes 3:7). Frederick Muhlenberg figured out which time it was when his church burned. When Israel came to Hebron to make David king, the men of Issachar came because they "had understanding of the times, to know what Israel ought to do" (1 Chronicles 12:32). Election Day is 3 weeks out, and the church needs people who understand the times.

**Know what time it is, because this is a time to speak.**

## Application
1. Honestly, have you been living like your faith and your vote belong to 2 separate kingdoms?
2. What is one step you could take before November 3rd to get involved in our city's election beyond casting a vote?

## Challenge
Look up the candidates for mayor of Harrison today and read what each one says they will do for our city. If there's a candidate forum before November 3rd, put it on your calendar and go. Frederick Muhlenberg waited until his church burned to get involved, and you don't have to wait that long.

## Prayer
Lord, you set every season under heaven, and you know what time it is even when I don't. Forgive me for treating my faith like it lives in one kingdom and my citizenship in another. Give me the understanding of the times you gave the men of Issachar, so I know what I ought to do. Teach me to recognize a time to speak when it's in front of me, and give me the courage to step into it before something has to burn. Amen.
