## Devotional
Is it sin to possess the privilege to vote and not vote? I ask that for true spiritual reflection, and I'd rather you answer it honestly than quickly. James settles it in one line, "So whoever knows the right thing to do and fails to do it, for him it is sin" (James 4:17). Jesus raised the stakes when He said, "Everyone to whom much was given, of him much will be required" (Luke 12:48). If you live in a country where you get a say in who governs you, you've been given much.

Let's get down to the truth. If the ballot says marriage is not defined as the Bible would define it, if the ballot says a child can choose their own gender, if the ballot says the murder of babies at any stage of life is acceptable, and it is life, are we allowed to be silent? Scripture gives us a hard no, because when God's people say nothing, our silence signs what we know is wrong. Here in Harrison the ballot also asks who will lead our city next, and that race matters more than a lot of us act like it does. A mayor sets the course for the police who answer our calls and the town our kids grow up in. Jesus told the Pharisees, "If you were blind, you would have no guilt; but now that you say, 'We see,' your guilt remains" (John 9:41).

Jesus wrote to the church in Thyatira and commended them, "I know your works, your love and faith and service and patient endurance" (Revelation 2:19). Then He said, "But I have this against you, that you tolerate that woman Jezebel" (2:20). The Greek word for tolerate is aphiēmi, to leave alone, to let it go. Thyatira let her be, and they looked to someone else to deal with her while they did nothing themselves. Jesus held it against the whole church.

God told Ezekiel, "Son of man, I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel" (Ezekiel 3:17). The watchman was the lookout posted high on the city wall, the one who could see trouble coming before anyone inside could. If he saw it and kept quiet, God said, "his blood I will require at your hand" (3:18). You have the Word of God, which means you can see what's coming. When you stay quiet at the dinner table and in the voting booth, you're the watchman who saw the enemy and never sounded the trumpet.

**Knowing what's right makes you responsible for doing it.**

## Application
1. Honestly, is there something on your ballot or in our city that you already know the right answer to, and you've been hoping someone else would handle it?
2. Who is one person you could talk with this week about what Scripture says on the questions in front of our town?

## Challenge
Make your plan to vote today. Decide whether you'll vote early or on November 3rd, find out where your polling place is, and put it on your calendar like an appointment you can't miss. Then ask one person in your family or your Life Group whether they have a plan too.

## Prayer
Lord, you've shown me the right thing to do in your Word, and you've told me that knowing it makes me responsible for it. Forgive me for every time I tolerated what you hate because I was waiting on someone else to deal with it. Make me a faithful watchman on the wall of my home and my city. Give me the courage to speak and the faithfulness to vote as someone who belongs to you. Amen.
