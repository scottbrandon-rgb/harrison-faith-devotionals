## Devotional
Daniel never went home. The last verse of chapter 1 says "Daniel was there until the first year of King Cyrus" (Daniel 1:21), which means he outlived Nebuchadnezzar and the entire Babylonian empire. He spent most of his life serving kings who didn't know his God, and he served them well. Daniel served Babylon, and he never belonged to it.

God gave the exiles their instructions before they ever settled in. "Seek the welfare of the city where I have sent you into exile, and pray to the LORD on its behalf, for in its welfare you will find your welfare" (Jeremiah 29:7). Notice who did the sending. God calls Babylon "the city where I have sent you," so even exile came with an assignment.

That's your assignment too. Paul reminded the Corinthians, "And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Corinthians 6:11). God rescues us out of Babylon, and then He sends us back in. The woman at the well went back to her town, and "many Samaritans from that town believed in him because of the woman's testimony" (John 4:39). The man Jesus freed from a legion of demons begged to get in the boat, and Jesus sent him home to tell how much the Lord had done for him (Mark 5:19). Jesus prayed for us the same way, "I do not ask that you take them out of the world, but that you keep them from the evil one" (John 17:15).

Every step this week has run through the mind. Babylon wanted Daniel's language, his literature, his lifestyle and his loyalty, and all 4 of those live in the mind. Paul answers with one command, "Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus" (Philippians 2:5). The Greek word is phroneo, a way of thinking that sets the direction of a whole life. Jesus had every right to stay in heaven. He "emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant" (Philippians 2:7), stepped into a world that rejected Him and came to save it anyway.

That's the God we worship. The Son of God lived in a world that wasn't His home, served the very people who would crucify Him and never once let that world live in Him. He walked into our Babylon to bring us out, and now He sends us back with His own mind. Daniel lived in Babylon for close to 70 years and Babylon never lived in him, and the God who kept Daniel is the same God who's keeping you.

**Babylon wants your mind, and Christ gives you His.**

## Application
1. Honestly, have you been more focused on escaping the culture around you or on serving the people in it?
2. Who is one person in your Babylon, at work or on your street, you could serve this week the way Jesus served you?

## Challenge
Pick one place you go every week, your job, your kid's school, the gym or the grocery store, and pray Jeremiah 29:7 over it today. Then do one specific thing to seek its welfare, something that costs you a little time or comfort. Pay attention to who God puts in front of you while you do it.

## Prayer
Jesus, you came into a world that rejected you, and you came to save it anyway. Thank you for washing me and then sending me back into the city with your mind. Make me like Daniel, faithful to serve and unwilling to belong to anything but you. Let Harrison be better because your people live here. Amen.
