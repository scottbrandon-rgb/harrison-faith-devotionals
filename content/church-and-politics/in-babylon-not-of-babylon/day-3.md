## Devotional
The king's food came with the job. Every day Daniel and his friends were handed "a daily portion of the food that the king ate, and of the wine that he drank" (Daniel 1:5). For a teenager a long way from home, it had to look like the best part of the deal. Daniel had already learned the language and sat through the literature, and he let both of those stand. The king's table was the one place he pushed back.

Some say the food wasn't kosher, and some say it had been offered to idols first. Solomon gives a sharper reason, "When you sit down to eat with a ruler, observe carefully what is before you, and put a knife to your throat if you are given to appetite. Do not desire his delicacies, for they are deceptive food" (Proverbs 23:1, 3). In the ancient world, sitting at a king's table meant entering into covenant with that king. You ate his bread, and you owed him your loyalty. That's why it cut David so deep when a close friend "who ate my bread" turned around and "lifted his heel against me" (Psalm 41:9), because bread meant belonging.

Food is just food to most of us. I can eat Taco Bell with you or a sandwich from the Bent and Dent, and it's no big deal. So picture going 2 weeks without a real meal, scraping by on whatever you could find, and then somebody sets a hot plate in front of you. Your whole disposition toward that person changes whether you mean for it to or not, because they just handed you life. The word for what happens next is dependence. Nebuchadnezzar was feeding them, schooling them, employing them and protecting them, and every one of those gifts tied them a little tighter to Babylon.

Sometimes the danger is in what the culture demands, and sometimes it's in what the culture offers. The offer is harder to see because it looks like provision. A paycheck that comes with an expectation that you'll keep quiet about your faith. A friend group that feeds your loneliness and starves your walk with God. A company or a government that promises to meet every need if you'll look to it first. Daniel asked the question you have to ask. What will accepting the king's offer require of me? Who's really feeding me? Whose table am I sitting at?

That's how Babylon moves in, one comfortable meal at a time, until the comfort itself holds you captive. Jesus set a different table, "I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger" (John 6:35). When He's the one feeding you, your loyalty is safe.

**Whoever sets your table will try to set your loyalty.**

## Application
1. Whose table have you been sitting at, and what is it quietly asking of you?
2. What is one comfort you could set down this week to remind yourself who really feeds you?

## Challenge
If your health allows, skip one meal today and spend that time in prayer. When the hunger hits, read John 6:35 and thank God out loud for 3 specific ways He has provided for you this year. Then write down one source of provision in your life that has started asking for your loyalty, and give it back to Him in prayer.

## Prayer
Father, you have fed me every day of my life, and every good thing on my table came from your hand. Forgive me for the times I've looked to a paycheck, a person or a system as if it were my provider. Give me Daniel's discernment to see what an offer will cost before I take it. Be my bread, and keep my loyalty with you. Amen.
