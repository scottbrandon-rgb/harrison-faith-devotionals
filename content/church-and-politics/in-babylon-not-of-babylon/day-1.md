## Devotional
Jesus told a story about a farmer who sowed good seed in his field, "but while his men were sleeping, his enemy came and sowed weeds among the wheat and went away" (Matthew 13:25). No one noticed until the plants came up and the weeds were standing right there beside the grain. Last week we talked about watchmen who fell asleep on the wall. This week we look at what grows while they sleep, because culture gets planted while we sleep, and Daniel 1 shows us exactly how Babylon plants it.

Around 605 BC, Nebuchadnezzar carried off the best young men of Judah, and Daniel was one of them, most likely still a teenager. The king's first order was a curriculum. He told his chief official to "teach them the literature and language of the Chaldeans" (Daniel 1:4). Before Babylon ever touched Daniel's food or his name, it went after his vocabulary. That's an old move for that city. Genesis says the tower was called Babel "because there the LORD confused the language of all the earth" (Genesis 11:9), and the Hebrew word behind it, balal, means to mix or to confuse. Babylon has been mixing up words from the very beginning.

Why start with language? Because if I can change the way you speak, I can change the way you think, and if I can change the way you think, I can change the way you live. You've watched it happen in your own lifetime. The Flintstones promised "a gay old time," and back then that word meant happy. Drunkenness became chemical dependency, which sounds a lot softer and a lot less like sin. A word gets a new meaning, the new meaning gets repeated, and after a while the old meaning sounds offensive and the new one sounds normal. Isaiah watched the same thing in his day, "Woe to those who call evil good and good evil, who put darkness for light and light for darkness" (Isaiah 5:20).

This matters for you because "death and life are in the power of the tongue, and those who love it will eat its fruits" (Proverbs 18:21). Whatever you keep saying, you start lining up with, and sooner or later you eat what you've been saying. When you use their terms, you start using their meanings. God has already defined sin, marriage, life and what it means to be a man or a woman, and we're not at liberty to redefine what He has defined.

Daniel did learn Chaldean. He read their books and held his own in the king's court, the same way Paul said, "I have become all things to all people, that by all means I might save some" (1 Corinthians 9:22). Daniel spoke Babylon's language for close to 70 years and kept every word God had said. He lived in Babylon his whole life, and Babylon never got to live in him.

**Learn to speak to Babylon without letting Babylon speak for you.**

## Application
1. Where have you started using the world's word for something God already calls sin?
2. What is one word or phrase you could drop this week and replace with the way Scripture says it?

## Challenge
Pay attention to your own words today, in conversation, in texts and in anything you post. Write down every word you catch yourself using that softens something God calls sin. Next to each one, write the word Scripture uses, and say that one out loud.

## Prayer
Father, you spoke the world into being, and your words still mean what you said they mean. Guard my mouth, because I've picked up more of Babylon's vocabulary than I realized. Teach me to speak to the people around me with grace, the way Daniel did, without ever trading your definitions for theirs. Let what comes out of my mouth bring life. Amen.
