## Devotional
The last thing Babylon went after was their names. "The chief of the eunuchs gave them names: Daniel he called Belteshazzar, Hananiah he called Shadrach, Mishael he called Meshach, and Azariah he called Abednego" (Daniel 1:7). Each of their Hebrew names carried the name of God, and Daniel's meant "God is my judge." The new names carried the gods of Babylon. Nebuchadnezzar was hanging a label on them that told everyone who they belonged to now.

Loyalty is a matter of identity. Whoever you belong to gets to tell you what you're worth, and when you're loyal to someone, you let them tell you who you are. Babylon wanted to be the one telling these four young men who they were, because once it had their loyalty, it could send them out to win others to Babylon.

Look closely at verses 7 and 8, because the Hebrew shows something the English hides. In verse 7, the chief of the eunuchs "gave them names." In verse 8, "Daniel resolved that he would not defile himself." Both phrases use the same Hebrew word, sim, which means to set or to place. The official set a name on Daniel, and Daniel set his heart. Babylon could set all kinds of things on him, a title, a uniform, a seat at the king's table, and only Daniel decided what got set in him. More than 60 years later, the queen still called him "this Daniel, whom the king named Belteshazzar" (Daniel 5:12).

We watched that happen in the baptismal tank on Sunday. We had 7 baptisms planned, and then an eighth man showed up who wanted to be baptized right then. Everyone had already changed out of their baptism clothes, so he and I jumped in the tank in our street clothes. He had the wrong outfit and the right heart, and the heart is what counts in that water. He went under in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit (Matthew 28:19), and he came up belonging to Jesus. God was in that place, and you could feel it.

That's what God says over everyone who belongs to Him, "Fear not, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name, you are mine. When you pass through the waters, I will be with you" (Isaiah 43:1, 2). Three of these young men would one day stand in a furnace under the names Babylon gave them, and the God who named them first walked into the fire with them. Babylon can hang any label it wants on the outside of you, and it only moves in when you let it set your heart.

**Set your heart before Babylon sets your name.**

## Application
1. Who have you allowed to name you, whether it's a past mistake, a job title or someone else's opinion of you?
2. What is one decision you could settle in your heart today, before the pressure ever shows up?

## Challenge
Write Isaiah 43:1 on a card and put your own name where it says Jacob. Keep it in your pocket or on your dash today, and every time you see it, say out loud whose you are. If you've never been baptized, talk to one of our pastors this week about taking that step.

## Prayer
Lord, you called me by name before anyone else had a chance to label me, and I am yours. Thank you for the 8 who went under the water on Sunday, and for the new name every one of them carries now. Set my heart the way you set Daniel's, so that no title or pressure can change whose I am. When I pass through the waters and the fire, be with me. Amen.
