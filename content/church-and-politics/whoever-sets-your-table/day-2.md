## Devotional
"Why should we die before your eyes, both we and our land?" (Genesis 47:19). That was the question the Egyptians brought to Joseph deep into the famine, and it's the question that set Pharaoh's table. Hunger had turned into the fear of dying, and the fear of death will make a person sign almost anything.

Look at where the grain came from. During the 7 good years Joseph had Pharaoh take a fifth of every harvest (Genesis 41:34), and the food was stored "in the cities" (Genesis 41:48). When the famine hit, Joseph "opened all the storehouses and sold to the Egyptians" (Genesis 41:56). The people paid for their own grain with their money, then their livestock, then their land, and finally with themselves, "Buy us and our land for food, and we with our land will be servants to Pharaoh." God used Joseph to keep a nation alive (Genesis 50:20), and the pattern in that storehouse still holds, because the government cannot give what it does not first take.

Listen to what the people said when it was over, "You have saved our lives; may it please my lord, we will be servants to Pharaoh" (Genesis 47:25). They were afraid enough to sign themselves over, and they said thank you while they did it. More than 3,000 years later Lenin described socialism with the same picture, "All citizens are transformed into hired employees of the state." Egypt had already lived it. Whoever sets your table will try to set your loyalty, and the man who held the grain ended up holding the people.

Then the story turns. "There arose a new king over Egypt, who did not know Joseph" (Exodus 1:8), and the servants of one Pharaoh became the slaves of the next, building his "store cities" out of brick and mortar (Exodus 1:11, 14). Whatever you hand to a throne out of fear stays with the throne, no matter who sits on it next. You'll see that fear in your own life every time something tells you that you won't make it unless you hand over a little more.

Fear always wants you running to the nearest table. The psalmist points you to a better one, "the eye of the LORD is on those who fear him, on those who hope in his steadfast love, that he may deliver their soul from death and keep them alive in famine" (Psalm 33:18, 19).

**Never let fear decide whose table you sit at.**

## Application
1. What fear has been pushing you toward a table God never set for you?
2. What is one fear you could say out loud to God today, trusting Him to keep you alive in your own famine?

## Challenge
Read Genesis 47:13 through 26 today and write down everything the Egyptians handed over, in the order they gave it. Next to that list, write the one fear in your life that keeps asking you to hand something over. Pray Psalm 33:18 and 19 over that fear before the day is done.

## Prayer
Father, you keep your people alive in famine, and your eye has never left me. Forgive me for the times I've let fear send me running to a table you didn't set. Deliver my soul from the fear of death, and keep my loyalty with you when the shelves look empty. Everything I have came from your hand first. Amen.
