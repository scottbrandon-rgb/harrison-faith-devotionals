## Devotional
Fear is one way to set a table, and free stuff is the other. Israel was out of Egypt and walking through the wilderness, and God was feeding them every single morning. They had everything they needed and still cried for what they wanted. "Now the rabble that was among them had a strong craving" (Numbers 11:4).

The Hebrew behind strong craving literally says they craved a craving. It started with the rabble, a word that comes from a root meaning to gather, and many scholars connect them to the "mixed multitude" that came out of Egypt alongside Israel (Exodus 12:38). The craving spread through the camp until "the people of Israel also wept again." By the end of the chapter the place got a name that means graves of craving, "because there they buried the people who had the craving" (Numbers 11:34). A craving that starts at the edge of the camp rarely stays at the edge.

Listen to what they remembered, "the fish we ate in Egypt that cost nothing, the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions, and the garlic" (Numbers 11:5). Those fish cost them their sons thrown into the Nile (Exodus 1:22), their backs under the taskmaster and generations of their freedom. They craved the good things of Egypt and forgot Egypt's price. A craving will do that to your memory, because it edits out the chains and keeps the cucumbers.

Then it got worse. "Let us choose a leader and go back to Egypt" (Numbers 14:4). They were ready to pick a new ruler and walk back into slavery to get the free stuff again. Free stuff builds dependence, and once you depend on a table you'll defend whoever sets it. Whoever sets your table will try to set your loyalty, and a craving is how that table gets you to pull up a chair on your own.

You know that pull. It's the offer that asks for nothing today and everything later. It's the purchase with no payments for 12 months, the relationship that fills your loneliness and empties your walk with God, and the promise from anyone in power that you'll never go without again as long as you look to them first. Jesus spoke straight to it, "Do not work for the food that perishes, but for the food that endures to eternal life, which the Son of Man will give to you" (John 6:27).

**When Egypt starts to look good, remember what it cost.**

## Application
1. What from your old life are you still craving, and what has it been costing you?
2. What is one free offer you could turn down this week because of what it would ask of you later?

## Challenge
Write down one thing you've been craving lately that God hasn't given you. Next to it, write what that thing would really cost you if you went back to Egypt to get it. Then read John 6:27 out loud and give that craving to God in prayer.

## Prayer
Lord, you fed Israel every morning in the wilderness, and you've fed me every day of my life. Forgive me for craving the good things of Egypt and forgetting the chains that came with them. Turn my appetite toward the food that endures to eternal life. Keep me from choosing a new leader and going back. Amen.
