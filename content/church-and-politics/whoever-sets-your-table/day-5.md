## Devotional
Luke ends the family tree of Jesus with a line most of us skim right past, "the son of Enos, the son of Seth, the son of Adam, the son of God" (Luke 3:38). Then he turns the page and puts Jesus in the wilderness for 40 days with nothing to eat, and the devil walks up with the same 2 offers Adam heard in the garden. "If you are the Son of God" goes after His identity. "Command this stone to become bread" (Luke 4:3) goes after His provision.

Adam took the food. Israel cried for Egypt's food. Jesus looked at a stone that could have been bread and turned it down. He answered with Israel's own wilderness verse, "Man shall not live by bread alone" (Luke 4:4), straight out of Deuteronomy 8:3. The first son of God lost the garden over one meal, and the Son of God won the wilderness by refusing one.

Then the devil showed Him every kingdom of the world and said, "To you I will give all this authority and their glory... If you, then, will worship me, it will all be yours" (Luke 4:6, 7). Satan offered back the dominion Adam had handed him, all of it, for one bow. Jesus answered, "You shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve" (Luke 4:8). He was quoting Deuteronomy 6:13, and the Hebrew word behind serve is abad, the same word from Pharaoh's Egypt. Even Jesus chose whom He would serve. Because He did, "all authority in heaven and on earth" belongs to Him (Matthew 28:18), and God has "seated us with him in the heavenly places" (Ephesians 2:6).

Pharaoh's table came through the fear of death and through free stuff, and Jesus defeated both. He beat free stuff in the wilderness when He turned down the bread, and He beat the fear of death on the cross, to "deliver all those who through fear of death were subject to lifelong slavery" (Hebrews 2:15). The Egyptians asked "Why should we die?" and sold themselves to Pharaoh, and Jesus died so you would never have to sell yourself to anyone. Whoever sets your table will try to set your loyalty, and the One who set yours paid for your seat with His own blood.

That's the God we worship. The same God who set the first table in the garden has set yours, and David tells us where He sets it, "You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies; you anoint my head with oil; my cup overflows" (Psalm 23:5). Whatever this year has held and whatever next year brings, no enemy standing around that table can clear it away. You're His now, and your Father has already filled your plate.

**Your Father already set the table, so stay seated.**

## Application
1. What other table have you been eyeing lately, hoping it will give you what you think God hasn't?
2. What would change this week if you actually believed God has set a table for you right in front of the thing you fear?

## Challenge
Read Psalm 23 out loud this morning and again before bed. When you get to verse 5, stop and thank God for one specific fear or pressure He has fed you in front of this year. Then spend a few minutes worshiping Him for who He is, without asking Him for anything.

## Prayer
Jesus, you looked at a stone that could have been bread and chose your Father's word instead. Thank you for refusing the devil's table so I could sit at yours. You've prepared a table before me in the presence of my enemies, and nothing can take away what you've given. I belong to you, and I'm staying seated with you. Amen.
