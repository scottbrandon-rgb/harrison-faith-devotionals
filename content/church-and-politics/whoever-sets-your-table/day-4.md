## Devotional
Pharaoh had a word for Israel, and it was slaves. God had another one. "Israel is my firstborn son... Let my son go that he may serve me" (Exodus 4:22, 23). The Hebrew word for serve there is abad, the same root behind "servants to Pharaoh" in Genesis 47 and "work as slaves" in Exodus 1. Everybody serves somebody, and God gave Israel the name son before He gave them their freedom or their food.

Then God did something that looks strange for a Father. Moses explained it 40 years later, "he humbled you and let you hunger and fed you with manna... that he might make you know that man does not live by bread alone, but man lives by every word that comes from the mouth of the LORD" (Deuteronomy 8:3). He let them get hungry on purpose. Pharaoh fed them to keep them, and God let them hunger to free them. The hunger was the classroom, and the lesson was where life really comes from.

Even the name of the manna was a question. When it first covered the ground, "they said to one another, 'What is it?'" (Exodus 16:15), and in Hebrew that's man hu, which is where the word manna comes from. Every morning they had to gather it exactly the way God said, enough for the day and double before the Sabbath, so He could "test them, whether they will walk in my law or not" (Exodus 16:4). The bread kept their bodies alive, and the word that came with it kept them alive as His people. God's word is life, and the manna preached that sermon every single morning for 40 years.

Daniel learned that lesson and carried it into Babylon. When the king's table was set in front of him, he asked instead, "Let us be given vegetables to eat and water to drink" (Daniel 1:12). At the end of 10 days "they were better in appearance and fatter in flesh than all the youths who ate the king's food" (Daniel 1:15). Daniel knew his life came from the word of the God who had fed his fathers in the wilderness, and the king's bread had nothing to add to it. Jesus finished the thought, "my Father gives you the true bread from heaven" (John 6:32), and then He told them, "I am the bread of life" (John 6:35).

That's why your Bible matters more than your breakfast. Whoever sets your table will try to set your loyalty, and the Father sets His table with His Word. Sons always have a seat at the Father's table, and the manna is still waiting on the ground every morning for whoever will go out and gather it.

**Gather the Word before you gather your breakfast.**

## Application
1. Honestly, when you're hungry for comfort or answers, what do you reach for before you reach for God's Word?
2. What would it look like to gather your manna every morning this week before you open anything else?

## Challenge
Before you eat or check your phone today, read Deuteronomy 8:1 through 10. Write verse 3 on a card and keep it where you eat. Do the same thing every morning for the rest of this week, and gather your manna first.

## Prayer
Father, you let me hunger so I would learn where life comes from. Forgive me for running to every other table before I come to yours. Teach me to live by every word that comes from your mouth, the way Daniel did in Babylon and the way Jesus did in the wilderness. Give me this day my daily bread, and let it start with your Word. Amen.
