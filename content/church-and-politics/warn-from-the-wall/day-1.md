## Devotional
Sunday night the farm was packed. A lot of you came out for Fall on the Farm, and it was a big day with a great atmosphere, and I'm thankful for every one of you who came. When you look across a crowd that size, you’re most likely  looking at people who will mark different ballots on November 3rd. Early voting opens today, Election Day is a little over 2 weeks out, and so here's the question for this week. Can the church stay that unified when the conversation turns to politics?

Paul faced the same question in Corinth. One group said, "I follow Paul," another said Apollos, another said Cephas, and Paul answered with a question of his own, "Is Christ divided?" (1 Corinthians 1:13). Then he gave them the picture of a body, one body with many members (1 Corinthians 12:12). The Greek word for member is melos, a limb, a working part of a living thing. You can be the right hand or the left hand and still belong to the same body. What makes you part of the body is whether you've submitted yourself to the Head, Jesus Christ. That's why Paul says "the eye cannot say to the hand, 'I have no need of you'" (1 Corinthians 12:21).

Unity also requires us to know which disagreements carry weight. Some things are matters of precept, where God has clearly spoken, like life in the womb, marriage and what it means to be male and female. Some are matters of prudence, where we agree on the principle and disagree on how to apply it, like foreign policy or what to do about gas prices. Some are simply preference, the candidates and parties and approaches we personally favor. Where God has spoken, we must stand together. Where wisdom is required, we should exercise humility. Where preference is involved, we must extend grace.

We get into trouble two ways. We take a preference, raise it to the level of Scripture, and then judge other believers by whether they agree with us. Or we take something God has clearly spoken, a precept, and shrink it down to a preference because it makes people uncomfortable. That latter feels peaceful, and that's what makes it dangerous. Ezekiel called out the prophets of his day for saying "'Peace,' when there is no peace" (Ezekiel 13:10). The Hebrew word there is shalom, which means whole, intact, nothing broken. A quiet home, bedroom, or classroom can still have a cracked wall.  This week we're going up on the wall to look at it.
**Where God has spoken, stand together, and where He hasn't, extend grace.**

## Application
1. Honestly, is there a political preference you've been treating like it came straight out of Scripture?
2. Who is one believer who votes differently than you that you could reach out to this week, just to tell them you're glad they're in the body?

## Challenge
Take a sheet of paper and write 3 headings across the top, conviction, prudence and preference. Put each race and question from your sample ballot under the heading where it belongs. Anything you put under conviction needs a verse written beside it, and if you can't find one, move it to another column.

## Prayer
Lord Jesus, you are the Head of this body, and every one of us belongs to you before we belong to anything else. Forgive me for the times I've treated my opinions like they were your Word, and for the times I've treated your Word like it was only an opinion. Teach me to stand firm where you have spoken and to be gracious where you haven't. Keep this church one body as November 3rd gets closer. Amen.
