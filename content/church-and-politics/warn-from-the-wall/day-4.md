## Devotional
Eli had been high priest at Shiloh for years, and his 2 sons, Hophni and Phinehas, served as priests beside him. They took the best of the sacrifices for themselves and slept with the women who served at the entrance of the tent of meeting (1 Samuel 2:22). Eli knew about it, and he said something. "Why do you do such things? For I hear of your evil dealings from all these people" (1 Samuel 2:23). The right words came out of his mouth, and God still judged his house "for the iniquity that he knew, because his sons were blaspheming God, and he did not restrain them" (1 Samuel 3:13). As high priest, Eli was the one man in Israel with the authority to remove them from the altar, and he never used it. Eli said the right words and still didn't use the authority he had.

So what are you supposed to do? Start where Paul starts. "First of all, then, I urge that supplications, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgivings be made for all people, for kings and all who are in high positions" (1 Timothy 2:1-2). Pray before you say a word to anyone. Then speak "the truth in love" (Ephesians 4:15), and speak where God has spoken. God condemned the prophets in Ezekiel's day "who prophesy from their own hearts" (Ezekiel 13:2), so save your strongest words for the places where He's been clear.

Next, use the authority you actually have. Some of you can't vote, maybe because you're too young, or because you live outside the city limits and can't vote for mayor. You still have a voice. Moms and dads, you have a table. "You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise" (Deuteronomy 6:7). How will we change the generation ahead of us if we aren't changing the conversations at our tables?

Then there's the ballot. No candidate can elect himself, and no mayor, governor, senator or president gets the job without somebody using their authority to put them there. God has placed us in a nation that hands its citizens that power, and early voting opened on Monday. Somebody will ask what one little vote matters. It matters when you stand before the Lord, because like the watchman in Ezekiel 33, you answer for whether you blew the trumpet, however big the army looked.

**Use the authority God has put in your hand.**

## Application
1. Where have you said the right words about something but never used the authority God gave you to change it?
2. What would it look like to make one conversation at your table this week about what God has said?

## Challenge
If you're registered, make your plan to vote today, whether that's early voting this week or on November 3rd, and put the time on your calendar. If you can't vote, pick one meal this week and bring Deuteronomy 6:7 to the table. Ask your family one question about what God has said, and let the conversation run.

## Prayer
Father, you have given me a voice, a table and in this season a vote, and none of them are mine to waste. Forgive me for the times I've said the right things and never acted on them. Teach me to pray first, to speak the truth in love, and to use every bit of authority you've put in my hands. Let my home be a place where your Word gets talked about when we sit, when we walk, when we lie down and when we rise. Amen.
