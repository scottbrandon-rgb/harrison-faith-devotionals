## Devotional
Thyatira was a working town. It sat on a trade road in western Asia Minor and was known for its guilds, the dyers, wool workers, leather workers, potters and bronze smiths. Lydia, the first convert in Europe, was "a seller of purple goods, from the city of Thyatira" (Acts 16:14). If you had a trade in Thyatira, you belonged to a guild, and if you didn't belong, nobody did business with you. The guilds held regular meals in honor of their patron gods, and the food on the table had been offered to idols. Your seat at that table was your paycheck.

Jesus had a lot of good to say about the church there. "I know your works, your love and faith and service and patient endurance, and that your latter works exceed the first" (Revelation 2:19). That sounds a lot like the American church. We've sent missionaries, planted churches and fed the hungry, and our latter works really do exceed the first. Then comes verse 20, "But I have this against you, that you tolerate that woman Jezebel." The Greek word for tolerate means to let something alone. They saw what she was teaching and decided not to deal with it, because dealing with it would have cost them their seat at the guild table.

Remember the original Jezebel. When King Ahab sulked over a vineyard he couldn't have, she said, "Do you now govern Israel? Arise and eat bread and let your heart be cheerful; I will give you the vineyard" (1 Kings 21:7). She handed him comfort and took his authority in the same breath. That's the exchange she always offers, a comfortable life in return for your voice. So let me ask you. Have we traded our convictions for our comforts? Are we quiet because there's nothing to say, or because saying it would cost us the table?

Ezekiel watched the prophets of his day do the same thing. "When the people build a wall, these prophets smear it with whitewash" (Ezekiel 13:10). The wall looked fine from the street, but it was cracked through, and God promised a storm that would knock it down until "its foundation will be laid bare" (13:14). Keeping quiet about an issue has never made it go away. Silence is plaster over the crack.

Isaiah had a farmer's picture for it. "His watchmen are blind; they are all without knowledge; they are all silent dogs; they cannot bark" (Isaiah 56:10). Some of you have a dog on the place right now, and you know exactly what he's for. A dog that won't bark can't protect the farm.

**Silence is plaster over a crack God still sees.**

## Application
1. Honestly, is there a conviction you've gone quiet on because speaking it could cost you a seat at some table?
2. What is one crack you've been plastering over, at home or in your own heart, that you could bring into the light this week?

## Challenge
Read the whole letter to Thyatira in Revelation 2:18-29 today. Write down the comforts in your life that would be at risk if you spoke up about what God has said. Pray over that list, and hand every item on it back to the Lord.

## Prayer
Lord Jesus, you see our works and you see what we tolerate. Forgive me for the times I've traded what you said for what made my life easier. Keep me from being a silent dog on the wall, and give me a bark that protects the people around me. Tear off whatever plaster I've put up, and build my life on a foundation that can stand your storm. Amen.
