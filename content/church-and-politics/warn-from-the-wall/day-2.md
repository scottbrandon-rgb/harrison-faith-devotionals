## Devotional
Ezekiel was living among the exiles in Babylon when God gave him chapter 33, and a few verses later a survivor would show up with the news, "The city has been struck down" (Ezekiel 33:21). Jerusalem was gone. Before that news ever reached him, God reminded Ezekiel what his job was. In the ancient world a city's watchman stood on the wall and kept his eyes on the horizon. The Hebrew word is tsopheh, one who looks out. When he saw an army coming, he blew the shofar, the ram's horn, and everyone inside the walls knew to get ready.

Look at who brings the danger in verse 2. "If I bring the sword upon a land." The sword is the Lord's judgment, and the watchman's job is to warn people it's coming. If the people hear the trumpet and ignore it, "his blood shall be upon himself" (33:5). Then God says something that should make every believer sit up straight. "But if the watchman sees the sword coming and does not blow the trumpet, so that the people are not warned, and the sword comes and takes any one of them, that person is taken away in his iniquity, but his blood I will require at the watchman's hand" (Ezekiel 33:6). The person who dies is taken away in his own sin, and God still holds the quiet watchman accountable. Even when people are guilty, the silent watchman shares the blood.

What is the watchman supposed to warn about? Verse 7 answers it. "Whenever you hear a word from my mouth, you shall give them warning from me." The watchman's message comes from God's mouth, and his own opinions stay off the wall. That means when we stay silent about something God has spoken, we're silencing the Word of God. Proverbs asks the person who claims he didn't know, "Does not he who weighs the heart perceive it? Does not he who keeps watch over your soul know it?" (Proverbs 24:12). God is the Watchman over the watchmen.

Paul knew how this works from the inside. He told a crowd in Jerusalem that when Stephen was stoned, "I myself was standing by and approving and watching over the garments of those who killed him" (Acts 22:20). All he did was hold the coats. The Greek word for approving is syneudokeō, to agree together with someone, and Luke used the same word back at the stoning itself, "And Saul approved of his execution" (Acts 8:1). Standing by is still standing with. The hope for the watchman is in verse 9, "But if you warn the wicked to turn from his way, and he does not turn from his way, that person shall die in his iniquity, but you will have delivered your soul."

**Blow the trumpet, and leave the response to God.**

## Application
1. Honestly, where have you seen danger coming in someone's life and said nothing because it felt easier?
2. What would it look like to say one thing God has clearly spoken, out loud, to someone who needs to hear it this week?

## Challenge
Read Ezekiel 33:1-9 slowly today. Write down one thing God has clearly spoken in His Word that you've been quiet about, whether that's at work, at home or online. Ask the Lord for the right moment and the right words, and when He gives you both, blow the trumpet.

## Prayer
Father, you have made your people watchmen, and you have put your Word in our mouths so we could warn the people we love. Forgive me for every time I've seen the sword coming and stayed quiet because quiet was comfortable. Give me the courage to blow the trumpet and the trust to leave the response with you. Let me speak where you have spoken, and keep my own opinions off the wall. Amen.
