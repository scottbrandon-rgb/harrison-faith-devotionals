## Devotional
If you're a Christian, how can you be against something Jesus never addressed? That question gets thrown at believers all the time, and it's built like a trap. Paul says our weapons "destroy arguments and every lofty opinion raised against the knowledge of God" (2 Corinthians 10:5). Our fight is with arguments, and the people making them are people we want to win to Christ. Yesterday we saw how the culture got flooded. Today we look at 3 arguments that came in with the flood and what the Bible says back.

The first argument is that Jesus never addressed homosexuality, and the answer is that He addressed it when He defined marriage. "He who created them from the beginning made them male and female," and "the two shall become one flesh" (Matthew 19:4, 5). God authored marriage, so He is the only one with the right to define it. That makes marriage the real topic, and homosexuality is one of many departures from it. Jesus also said that out of the heart come "evil thoughts, sexual immorality, theft, murder, adultery" (Mark 7:21). The Greek word for sexual immorality is porneia, and to a Jew in Jesus' day it covered every sexual act forbidden in Leviticus 18. Beyond that, Jesus is "the Word" who "became flesh" (John 1:14), which means He is the author of Leviticus. If He has to mention something by name before it counts, then where did He address cocaine or sex trafficking?

The second argument grants that Paul said something and then shrinks it. It claims Paul only meant pederasty, older men using boys while keeping a wife at home, so a faithful, committed relationship would be fine. Romans 1 sinks that idea quickly, because Paul starts with women: "their women exchanged natural relations for those that are contrary to nature" (Romans 1:26). If Paul only meant men and boys, what was he talking about with the women? Then look at the word Paul uses in 1 Corinthians 6:9 for "men who practice homosexuality." It's arsenokoitai, and it looks like Paul built it from the Greek translation of Leviticus 18:22, combining arsēn, "male," and koitē, "bed." That verse says, "You shall not lie with a male as with a woman; it is an abomination." When Paul needed a word, he reached all the way back to Leviticus.

The third argument fits on a church sign, and I saw it on one. Driving through Branson, I passed a church with a sign that said, "Hate divides, and love unites." It sounds nice until you hold it next to Jesus: "Do not think that I have come to bring peace to the earth. I have not come to bring peace, but a sword" (Matthew 10:34). Paul says love "does not rejoice at wrongdoing, but rejoices with the truth" (1 Corinthians 13:6), and John says, "All wrongdoing is sin" (1 John 5:17). So love does divide. It cuts us off from whatever would separate us from Christ, and then it goes after the people still on the other side. Love seeks to reconcile what is already divided.

At the break room table, lunch table, and dinner table...your assignment is to speak "the truth in love" (Ephesians 4:15), and the results belong to God. The world says, "This is who you are," and love is willing to tell someone the truth about who God made them to be. Truth without love is destructive, love without truth is deceptive, and you need both when this subject comes up at work or at the dinner table.

**Marriage is the topic, Paul and Jesus both reached for Leviticus 18 and since Jesus is love, He alone gets to define it.**

## Application
1. Which of these 3 arguments has quietly made you less sure of what the Bible says?
2. Who is one person you could talk with this week, with truth and with kindness, if the subject comes up?

## Challenge
Read Matthew 19, verses 3 to 9, and Leviticus 18:22 side by side. Then write a 2 sentence answer, in your own words, to the claim that Jesus never addressed homosexuality. Keep it on your phone so you're ready to answer gently when someone asks.

## Prayer
Jesus, you are the Word who was there in the beginning, and you have already said what marriage is. Take every thought in me captive and make me obedient to you. Where the culture's arguments have made me unsure, settle me in your truth. Give me the courage to speak and the kindness to speak it well, so the people I talk with hear your heart and your Word together. Amen.
