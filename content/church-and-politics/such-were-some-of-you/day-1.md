## Devotional
A few Sundays ago, people walking out of church stopped to tell us an alarm was going off somewhere on the property. We didn't know which alarm it was, so we went looking, and it turned out to be the septic tank. When we opened the lid, the tank was almost full. A septic alarm sounds when a tank is close to its limit, and if we had ignored it much longer, we would have had a real mess on our hands.

That alarm is the picture Paul paints in Romans 1, and it's where we started on Sunday as we closed out our Church and Politics series. Paul says 3 times that "God gave them up" (Romans 1:24, 26, 28), and each time the slide goes a little deeper. It starts when people "exchanged the truth about God for a lie and worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator" (Romans 1:25). Only after that exchange does Paul describe women and men giving up natural relations for what is "contrary to nature" (Romans 1:26). So homosexuality shows up late in Paul's progression, and that makes it a warning light. It's how you know the fall has already started.

History reads the same way, even when the historian has no Bible open. J. D. Unwin held no religious conviction, and in the 1930s he studied 80 tribes and the great civilizations, Athens and Rome among them. His conclusion was blunt: "Any human society is free to choose either to display great energy or to enjoy sexual freedom; the evidence is that it cannot do both for more than one generation." Pitirim Sorokin, who founded Harvard's sociology department, found no example of a community that kept its place after looser sexual customs replaced stricter ones. Russia ran the experiment in real time. After the 1917 revolution the Bolsheviks loosened marriage and the laws around sex to break the family, and the damage was so bad that Joseph Stalin put many of those laws back.

That brings us to the hardest verse in the chapter. Every culture in every century has had sexual sin, so Paul locates the bottom somewhere else. In verse 32 he says people "not only do them but give approval to those who practice them." The bottom is the applause. God showed Abraham the same principle centuries earlier, when He told him his descendants would wait 400 years to take the land, "for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet complete" (Genesis 15:16). God was patient while that tank filled. When a culture moves from doing a sin to celebrating it, the alarm is telling us the tank is nearly full.

Before we point at anybody, though, watch where Paul goes next. The very next line says, "Therefore you have no excuse, O man, every one of you who judges" (Romans 2:1). Homosexuality sits in that tank beside easy divorce, adultery made respectable, and abortion, and the church has made its own deposits. This series opened with Ezekiel's watchman, the man responsible to blow the trumpet when he saw the sword coming (Ezekiel 33:6). Now the alarm is going off in our nation, and you get to decide whether you treat it like noise or get up and check the tank, starting with your own heart.

**Homosexuality isn't where the fall starts. It's how you know the fall has started.**

## Application
1. Where have you heard an alarm go off in your own heart and decided it was just noise?
2. What would it look like to check your own tank this week before you comment on anyone else's?

## Challenge
Read Romans 1:18 through 2:1 in one sitting and underline every place Paul says "God gave them up." Then ask the Lord to show you one place you've given approval to something He calls sin, whether it's a show you kept watching, a joke you laughed at, or a silence you kept. Confess it to Him today.

## Prayer
Father, you are patient, and you have waited on nations longer than I have been alive. Thank you for the alarms, even the ones I don't want to hear. Show me where my own heart has filled up with what you call sin, and where I have applauded what grieves you. Make me a watchman who blows the trumpet out of love, and keep me from being the one who hears the alarm and rolls over. Amen.
