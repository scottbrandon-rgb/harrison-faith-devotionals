## Devotional
Last Friday the FAA grounded every flight in the country. The communication and radar feeds controllers depend on became unstable, and the agency decided nothing would take off until it could trust what it was seeing. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed a newly installed router that was firing about 1,000 connection attempts every 4 or 5 seconds, flooding the network until messages couldn't get through clean. The FAA says the root cause is still under investigation. Either way, about 3,500 planes came down safely because someone decided an unstable system was not safe to fly.

A flood like that is a good picture of what happened to our culture, and on Sunday we traced how it happened. In 1948 Alfred Kinsey published his study on sexuality and reported that up to half of farm boys had sexual relations with animals. If that doesn't sound like your granddad, you already know the numbers were cooked. His samples leaned heavily on prisoners and prostitutes, and later in the 1970s it was discovered that Kinsey disregarded 75% of his research and chose not to make it public. His work still shaped the Model Penal Code, where consent replaced Scripture as the measuring stick. From there homosexual practice moved from a crime, to a condition we felt sorry for, to an identity people say they were created with, and it took 67 years to get from Kinsey to the Supreme Court redefining marriage in 2015. Even the old legal phrase "crime against nature" came from Romans 1:26. Isaiah has a word for a people who rename sin: "Woe to those who call evil good and good evil" (Isaiah 5:20).

That shift had a plan behind it, and the men who drew it up also published it in a book. In 1988 about 175 activist leaders met in Warrenton, Virginia, and the next year 2 Harvard trained men, one in psychology and one in marketing, published After the Ball. They called their method "a program of unabashed propaganda" and laid it out in 3 steps: desensitize, jam, and convert. Desensitizing meant flooding the country with images until it stopped reacting. In their own words, "If straights can't shut off the shower, they may at least eventually get used to being wet." Jamming meant tying every objection to something people already hate, so a pastor would feel like he was siding with the Nazis for preaching Romans 1.

Now go back to that FAA network, because it shows how those 2 steps work together. Desensitizing is the flood, a thousand connection attempts every few seconds until the volume itself feels normal. Jamming is what the flood does to the signal. Messages come through garbled, and truth starts to sound like hate. The goal was a people who can't read their own instruments.

I felt that last week. Julie and I celebrated our 17th anniversary in Branson, and along the way I noticed homosexual couples all around us, enjoying the same shows and the same restaurants we were. What caught me was how little it caught me. It's easy to walk right past a lifestyle that is really a death style, because Paul says it leads to death (Romans 1:32), and it's easy because it looks familiar. It looks familiar because we've been standing in the shower for almost 40 years. Jeremiah described a people who "did not know how to blush" (Jeremiah 6:15), and Paul described people who had "become callous" (Ephesians 4:19). Skin gets hard right where it rubs the most. Those couples are people Christ died for, and our fight is with "the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly places" (Ephesians 6:12), so the callus I'm worried about is the one on my own heart.

That's why the FAA's decision preaches. When the system was flooded, they called for a full ground stop, stopped everything and brought every plane down until they could trust the signal again. That's exactly what we need to do as a nation and as believers, shut off the noise until we can trust the signal again. How? Ask the Lord to consecrate you again, to set you apart, and to make your spirit sensitive to what He calls sin so you don't slowly accept what you used to grieve. We are not ignorant of his designs (2 Corinthians 2:11).

**When your spirit stops blushing, it's time for a ground stop.**

## Application
1. What used to grieve you that you now scroll past without a second thought?
2. What is one input you could shut off this week so you can hear the Lord clearly again?

## Challenge
Take a ground stop today. Pick one stretch of time, an evening or a lunch break, and turn off every screen and feed. Read Ephesians 4, verses 17 to 24, and ask the Holy Spirit to show you where your heart has grown callous. Write down one thing you've gotten used to that you need God to make you sensitive to again.

## Prayer
Lord, I've been standing in the shower so long that I forgot I was getting wet. Consecrate me again and set me apart for you. Where I've grown callous, make me tender, and where I've stopped blushing, give me back a conscience that grieves what grieves you. Keep me from accepting what you call sin, and keep me loving the people caught in it. Amen.
