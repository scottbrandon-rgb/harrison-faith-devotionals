## Devotional
Corinth had a reputation long before Paul ever arrived. Centuries earlier, Greek writers turned the city's name into a verb, korinthiazesthai, "to act like a Corinthian," and it meant to live in sexual immorality. The church Paul wrote to was made up of people who came out of that city, and his list in 1 Corinthians 6 reads like a walk down their old streets: "the sexually immoral, nor idolaters, nor adulterers, nor men who practice homosexuality, nor thieves, nor the greedy, nor drunkards, nor revilers, nor swindlers" (1 Corinthians 6:9, 10). Look closely and you'll find the greedy and the revilers in the same list as everyone else, which means every one of us can find our old address in there somewhere.

Then Paul writes 4 words that change the tense of the whole passage: "And such were some of you" (1 Corinthians 6:11). Were is past tense. All week we've said it this way: the world says, "This is who you are," and God says, "That is who you were." This verse is also proof that homosexuality is not immutable, because the people Paul wrote to changed. Men who once fit that list were sitting in the church at Corinth, and Paul could speak about who they used to be.

That changes how you see your struggle. Salvation is a matter of position, which is your identity, and sanctification is a matter of practice, which is your imitation of Jesus. The Corinthians were still fighting old habits, and Paul treats that fight as a sanctification issue, because the salvation issue had already been settled. "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come" (2 Corinthians 5:17). Until you can imitate Him completely, keep walking in your identity, and don't let your lack of imitation tell you who you are. A struggle shows you what to fight, and only the cross gets to tell you who you are.

If that's you, or your son, or your daughter, hear verse 11 before anything else from this week. "You were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified." Those are the same words Paul uses for the bride of Christ, that He might "sanctify her, having cleansed her by the washing of water with the word" (Ephesians 5:26). What God does for His bride, He will do for you. Look at who does the work, too: it happens "in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God." The Father, the Son, and the Spirit all take part in washing one sinner clean.

This series started with a watchman on a wall, and this week started with an alarm going off. It ends with the sound of water. The same God who sounds the alarm is the God who washes, sanctifies, and justifies, and that's worth worshiping Him for today. So hold on to 3 phrases as we close: don't be deceived (1 Corinthians 6:9), don't be ashamed (Romans 1:16), and be free, because "the truth will set you free" (John 8:32).

**Live like who you are, and leave who you were in the past tense.**

## Application
1. Is there a struggle you've let become the way you describe yourself?
2. Who in your life needs to hear verse 11 from you, and what would it look like to tell them this week?

## Challenge
Write 1 Corinthians 6:11 on a card and read it out loud 3 times today, putting your own name in front of each phrase. Then spend 5 minutes thanking God for what He has washed away in your life. Be specific, and let worship be the last word of your week.

## Prayer
Father, thank you that you never leave sinners where you find them. You washed me, you set me apart, and you declared me righteous in the name of Jesus and by your Spirit. When my struggle tries to tell me who I am, remind me who you say I am. Keep me from being deceived, keep me from being ashamed, and keep me free in the truth of your Word. Amen.
