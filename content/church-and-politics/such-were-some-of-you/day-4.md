## Devotional
Why does God care so much about what happens in a bedroom? Paul answers that in one sentence: "This is the will of God, your sanctification: that you abstain from sexual immorality" (1 Thessalonians 4:3). The Greek word for sanctify is hagiazō, and it means to set something apart for God's special use and make it distinct from what is common. Paul ties holiness straight to sexuality, which tells us God sees the bedroom as one of the main places He makes His people different from the world around them. Yesterday we answered the culture's arguments. Today we stand on God's design, because on Sunday we said His design for marriage has 3 purposes: procreation, illustration, and sanctification. The first 2 set up the third.

Procreation goes all the way back to the garden, where God blessed the man and woman and said, "Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth" (Genesis 1:28). Malachi asks the question plainly: "And what was the one God seeking? Godly offspring" (Malachi 2:15). Illustration comes from Paul, who quotes Genesis and then says, "This mystery is profound, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church" (Ephesians 5:32). A husband and wife are a living picture of Jesus and His bride. Two men or two women can't produce a child, and so they can't picture what God designed marriage to picture. If you and your spouse have never been able to have children, hear this clearly. God designed marriage around male and female, and your marriage still pictures Christ and the church.

Sanctification is where this gets personal for every one of us, married or single. Paul writes, "Because of the temptation to sexual immorality, each man should have his own wife and each woman her own husband" (1 Corinthians 7:2). Marriage gives desire a home. God knows you have desires, and He tells you where they belong. That's why Hebrews says, "Let the marriage bed be undefiled" (Hebrews 13:4). In marriage sex is set apart, kept for one man and one woman in covenant, and that makes it holy.

Marriage also sets you apart. Paul tells husbands to love their wives "as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her, that he might sanctify her" (Ephesians 5:25, 26). Marriage is where God sands off your selfishness. It looks like a husband who lays down what he wants after a long day, a wife who forgives the same thing for the third time, and 2 people who stay when walking away would be easier. If you're single, your body is just as set apart for God today, and your sanctification has already started.

So here's why it has to be God's design. You cannot be set apart for God by the very thing God has called sin. A union God calls "contrary to nature" (Romans 1:26) can't make anybody holy, because you can't make holy what God has already said is unholy. The world says, "This is who you are, so follow your desire." God says your desire needs a home, and He has already built one. Our job is to stand on that design and live it out well enough that the people watching can see it working.

**Desire needs a home, and God already built one.**

## Application
1. Where are you letting your desires tell you who you are instead of asking God where they belong?
2. What is one way you could lay your life down for your spouse, or guard your body for God, this week?

## Challenge
If you're married, ask your spouse this question tonight: "Where is God using me to make you more like Jesus, and where am I getting in the way?" Listen without defending yourself. If you're single, write 1 Thessalonians 4:3 to 4 on a card and put it somewhere you'll see it every night this week.

## Prayer
Father, you designed marriage before sin ever touched the world, and your design is good. Set me apart in my body, in my desires, and in the way I love the people closest to me. Sand off my selfishness and make me look more like Jesus. Help me honor marriage in what I do, what I watch, and what I celebrate, so the world can see your design working. Amen.
