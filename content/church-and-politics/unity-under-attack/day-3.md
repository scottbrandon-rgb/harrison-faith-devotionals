## Devotional
When the Pilgrims landed at Plymouth in 1620, they were bound by an agreement with the investors who paid for the voyage. For 7 years everything they grew, caught, or built went into "the common stock," and everyone ate out of it. Nobody owned a field. Nobody planted for his own family. The idea came from a dream as old as Plato, a society where everything is shared and everyone ends up equal.

Roughly half of them died that first winter, and hunger hung over the years after. Governor William Bradford wrote down what the common stock did to them. The young men resented working for other men's wives and children. The strong man got the same share as the man who couldn't do a quarter of his work. The older men felt disrespected. The wives called cooking and washing for other men a kind of slavery. A plan designed to make everyone equal ended up turning everyone against everyone else, and that's the irony of every human attempt to force people into unity: it breeds the very discord it promised to cure.

In the spring of 1623, Bradford gave up on the experiment and assigned each family its own parcel of land. The result surprised even him. Families planted far more corn than anyone could have ordered them to, and women who used to claim they were too weak to work went willingly into the fields with their children beside them. Bradford's verdict was blunt. The experiment proved "the emptiness of the theory of Plato," he wrote, as if Plato and his admirers "were wiser than God."

You've heard "wiser than God" before. It's what the serpent offered in the garden, "you will be like God" (Genesis 3:5), the idea that we can improve on what He designed. Paul answers it in one line: "the foolishness of God is wiser than men" (1 Corinthians 1:25). God's design for work, for property, for family, and for marriage was never up for an upgrade, no matter how good our theory sounds.

We all run our own small experiments where we're sure we've found a better way than His. There's the budget that skips the tithe, the relationship that skips the covenant, and the Sunday that skips worship because the week was long. They usually look smart for a season, the same way the common stock looked smart on paper back in England. Ask the Pilgrims how the season ends.

**When your plan sounds smarter than God's, assume it isn't.**

## Application
1. Where have you quietly decided you know better than God about how your life should work?
2. What would it look like to put that one area back under God's design this week?

## Challenge
Take the area you just thought of, whether it's money, marriage, work, or rest, and find one verse that speaks to it. Write it on a card or in your phone and read it every morning this week. Then take one step today that lines up with God's design over your own theory.

## Prayer
Father, your foolishness is wiser than my best ideas, and your weakness is stronger than my strongest plans. Forgive me for the places I've acted as if I were wiser than you. Bring my money, my marriage, my work, and my rest back under your design. Teach me to trust what you made more than anything I can come up with. Amen.
