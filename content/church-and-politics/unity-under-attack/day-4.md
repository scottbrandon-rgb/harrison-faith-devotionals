## Devotional
Abimelech ran for king on his bloodline. After Gideon died, this son of Gideon's concubine walked into Shechem, his mother's hometown, and asked one question: would you rather be ruled by 70 of Gideon's sons, or by one man? Then he added the line that sealed it, "Remember also that I am your bone and your flesh" (Judges 9:2). The leaders of Shechem never asked about his character. They said, "He is our brother" (Judges 9:3), and handed him 70 pieces of silver.

He used the money to hire "worthless and reckless fellows" (Judges 9:4), and they killed his brothers, 70 men on one stone. Sunday I called it the first instance of identity politics. Shechem picked a leader because he was one of theirs, and it cost them everything. That's the playbook Jesus warned about, "If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand" (Mark 3:24). Sort people into tribes, convince each tribe the other one is the enemy, and you'll never have to conquer anybody, because they'll hand you the keys.

A week after the election, the pressure to sort people is everywhere. Your feed wants you to see half the country as the enemy. The family group text wants you to pick a side before Thanksgiving. Somebody at church voted differently than you did, and the whisper says you can't trust them anymore. Sunday we heard a radical admit, "The issue is never the issue." The goal is always to get brothers to stop seeing each other as brothers.

One of Gideon's sons got away. Jotham, the youngest, hid during the massacre (Judges 9:5), and when he heard what Shechem had done, he climbed to the top of Mount Gerizim and "cried aloud" (Judges 9:7). Gerizim was the mountain where God told Israel to speak His blessing over the land (Deuteronomy 11:29), and Jotham stood on it and told Shechem the truth while everyone else went along. Pastor Martin Niemöller learned in Germany what happens when nobody does that. By the time they came for him, "no one was left to speak up."

Turnout was low on election day, which means a lot of people let somebody else decide. Staying home is still a choice, and it hands the decision to whoever showed up. So use your vote and use your voice, because God put you on that mountain for a reason. Just never let anyone choose your enemies for you on the way up.

**Use your voice, and never let anyone pick your enemies for you.**

## Application
1. Honestly, is there someone you've started seeing as the other side instead of as your brother or sister?
2. What would it look like to use your voice this week, at the ballot box, at work, or at the dinner table, without tearing anyone down?

## Challenge
Reach out to one believer you know voted differently than you and tell them you're glad they're in the family of God. Leave the election out of it unless they bring it up. Then look up the next date you can vote, whether it's a local race or a runoff, and put it on your calendar today.

## Prayer
Lord, you are the God of Jotham, who spoke up when everyone else went along. Give me courage to use my voice and my vote, and give me grace to do it without making enemies of my brothers and sisters. Guard my heart from every whisper that wants to sort your people into tribes. Keep this church one, because a house divided cannot stand. Amen.
