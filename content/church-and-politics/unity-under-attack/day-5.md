## Devotional
Herod's temple had a low stone wall running around its inner courts, with warning signs posted in Greek and Latin. One of those signs was dug up in Jerusalem in 1871, and it still reads like a threat: any foreigner who went past the barrier would have himself to blame for his own death. Paul knew that wall personally. He was arrested in Jerusalem because a crowd thought he'd brought a Gentile named Trophimus past it (Acts 21:28, 29), and when he wrote Ephesians, he wrote it as a prisoner (Ephesians 3:1).

So Paul had that wall in mind when he wrote that Jesus "has broken down in his flesh the dividing wall of hostility" (Ephesians 2:14). The Greek word for dividing wall is mesotoichon, the middle wall, the barrier in between. Every separation we've traced this week sits behind that word: a man hiding from his wife, Cain standing over Abel, Shechem sorting brothers by bloodline, and underneath all of it, people hiding from God among the trees. Sunday I said that separation from God is what condemns a person. Jesus took that wall down with His own body on the cross, and He made us one with the Father and one with each other.

Some of you woke up the morning after the election relieved, and some of you woke up worried about your kids and your country. Either way, nothing that happened at the ballot box built that wall, and nothing that happens there can put it back up. We should vote and we should speak, and we should keep our hope where Paul kept his, in the One who "himself is our peace" (Ephesians 2:14).

That's why Paul tells us to be "eager to maintain the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace" (Ephesians 4:3). The word is maintain, which means the unity already exists. Christ won it, and our job is to guard it. "There is one body and one Spirit" (Ephesians 4:4), so we stand in one spirit, serve as one body, share one life, follow one book, give for one purpose, and build one another.

The week started with 2 people hiding from God among the trees, and it ends here: "through him we both have access in one Spirit to the Father" (Ephesians 2:18). Whatever the whisper tells you this week, the wall is down, the door is open, and your Father is glad to see you.

**Christ already tore the wall down, so don't help anyone build it back.**

## Application
1. Where have you been putting more hope in an election, a leader, or an outcome than in Christ?
2. What is one way you could guard the unity of your church family this week?

## Challenge
Read Ephesians 2 out loud this morning. Then spend 5 minutes worshiping Jesus for breaking down the wall between you and God, without asking Him for anything. Before Sunday, do one thing that builds up someone in our church family, whether that's a text, a meal, or praying with them.

## Prayer
Jesus, you are my peace. You broke down the wall between me and the Father with your own body, and no vote, no leader, and no headline can build it back. Thank you for making me one with you and one with your people. Make me eager to keep the unity you paid for. Amen.
