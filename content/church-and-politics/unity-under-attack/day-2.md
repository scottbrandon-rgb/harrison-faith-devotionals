## Devotional
The first murder in the Bible happened in a field with nobody watching (Genesis 4:8). When God asked Cain where his brother was, Cain shrugged it off, "I do not know; am I my brother's keeper?" (Genesis 4:9). As far as Cain could tell there were no witnesses, and a crime nobody sees is a crime nobody answers for.

God answered him with a sentence that should settle that question for every one of us: "The voice of your brother's blood is crying to me from the ground" (Genesis 4:10). The Hebrew word for crying is tsa'aq, the cry of someone who's been wronged and is calling out for a judge, the same word God uses when He hears Israel groaning under Pharaoh's taskmasters (Exodus 3:7). Abel couldn't speak, so his blood did, and it went straight to the Lord. Every injustice on earth has an address, and it's heaven.

That's what Sunday's sermon called virtue, moral excellence that comes from living like God sees, because He does. Hebrews says "no creature is hidden from his sight, but all are naked and exposed to the eyes of him to whom we must give account" (Hebrews 4:13). A people who believe that will do right in secret, and a nation full of people like that doesn't need much ruling. Plato saw the other side of it 400 years before Christ: the less virtue inside a people, the more ruler they need outside. If nobody's watching, somebody has to be. If God isn't the judge, the state will volunteer for the job. If conscience goes quiet, the police get busy.

The same truth runs through every marriage in this church. Malachi tells the men of Israel that "the LORD was witness between you and the wife of your youth" (Malachi 2:14), which means a covenant takes 3, 2 people and a Witness. A covenant says, I'll keep my end because I told God I would. A contract takes God out and says, if you do this, I'll do that. Contracts break the first time somebody fails, and somebody always fails. Covenants hold, because the reason you keep your word was never the other person's performance in the first place.

So ask yourself who you're really accountable to, in your marriage, at your job, in the comment section. If the answer is the people around you, they'll let you down, and you'll feel free to let them down right back. If the answer is the Lord, you can keep your promises on the days nobody else keeps theirs, because the Witness is still in the room.

**Keep your word like the Witness is in the room, because He is.**

## Application
1. When have you kept your promise only as long as the other person kept theirs?
2. What is one promise you've let slide that you could keep this week, whether anyone notices or not?

## Challenge
Pick one thing you'd do differently if you were sure no one would ever find out, and settle it with God today. If you're married, tell your spouse one way you plan to keep your covenant this week, no matter how the week goes. If you're single, do the same with a friend or family member you've made a promise to.

## Prayer
Lord, you heard Abel's blood from the ground, and nothing I do is hidden from your sight. Make me the same person in secret that I am in public. Make me a person who keeps covenant when it costs something and when no one else is keeping theirs. Let my faithfulness at home and at work come from my word to you. Amen.
